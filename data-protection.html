Do You Need to Register?

Who the NDPC treats as “of major importance”

Under the NDPC’s registration guidance, you must register if you keep or have access to a filing system of personal data (paper or digital) and any of these applies:

You must register if

  • You process the personal data of more than 200 people (customers, staff, students, patients, users) in any six months.
  • You provide commercial ICT services on devices that belong to other people, such as phone or computer repair.
  • You operate in any of these sectors: aviation, communication, education, electric power, export and import, financial, health, hospitality, insurance, oil and gas, tourism, e-commerce or public service.

You do not need to register if

  • Traders and artisans who do not pass personal data on to other businesses as part of their trade.
  • Traders with fewer than 15 employees, and artisans, who keep no customer filing system beyond routine contacts, receipts and addresses.
  • Exempt by the NDPC: community-based associations, faith-based organisations, embassies and high commissions, courts and tribunals, and multigovernmental organisations.

Not registering does not take you outside the NDPA: you still need a lawful basis, a privacy notice and proper security for any personal data you hold.

The NDPC registration period has closed. Organisations registering now pay a 50% late penalty on top of the registration fee, and every day without registration adds to your regulatory risk. Register now rather than wait for the next cycle.

Newly incorporated company? The NDPC waives late registration penalties for companies incorporated no more than six months before they apply. The waiver is requested by letter with the Certificate of Incorporation; we prepare and send it with your registration.

Categories and Official Fees

Your category decides your NDPC fee and duties

Fees are paid to the NDPC, not to us, and are renewed every year. Ultra-High Level controllers also pay ₦5,000 a year for each data processor they engage.

Ultra-High Level (UHL)₦250,000 a yearCommercial banks, telecoms, insurers, multinationals, electricity distribution, oil and gas, payment gateways and fintechs, public social media and email apps, device manufacturers, and any organisation processing data of more than 5,000 people in six months.
Extra-High Level (EHL)₦100,000 a yearGovernment ministries, departments and agencies, microfinance and mortgage banks, higher institutions, secondary and tertiary hospitals, and any organisation processing data of 1,000 to 5,000 people in six months.
Ordinary-High Level (OHL)₦10,000 a yearPrimary and secondary schools, corporate training providers, primary health centres, independent medical laboratories, hotels and guest houses with fewer than 50 rooms, and any organisation processing data of 200 to 1,000 people in six months.

DPCO Licence

What the NDPC asks of a DPCO applicant

A Data Protection Compliance Organisation is an entity licensed by the NDPC under section 33 of the NDPA to train, audit, consult and render services that ensure compliance. Professional service consultancies, IT service providers, audit firms and law firms can apply, with staff qualified in areas such as data protection and privacy, information security, cybersecurity, data governance or GDPR compliance.

Documents for licensing

  • CAC registration
  • Evidence of tax clearance
  • Relevant professional or academic qualification of at least two listed staff (they need not be directors)
  • Valid identification of one director
  • A website on a .ng domain
  • Payment of the NDPC licensing fee

Source: NDPC, DPCO Registration & Requirements.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does my small business have to register with the NDPC?

Only if you are a data controller or processor of major importance: more than 200 people’s data in six months, ICT services on other people’s devices, or one of the listed sectors. Small traders and artisans without a customer filing system are not of major importance. Either way, the rest of the NDPA still applies to you.

Is there a penalty for registering late?

Yes. The NDPC registration period has closed, and registrations now attract a 50% late penalty on top of the fee. Companies incorporated no more than six months before applying can ask the NDPC to waive the late penalty, by letter with their Certificate of Incorporation. We handle that request as part of your registration.

How much does NDPC registration cost?

The NDPC fee is ₦250,000 for Ultra-High Level, ₦100,000 for Extra-High Level and ₦10,000 for Ordinary-High Level organisations, renewed yearly, plus a 50% late penalty now that the registration period has closed (new companies can apply for a waiver). Ultra-High Level controllers also pay ₦5,000 for each data processor they engage in a year. Our professional fee is separate and quoted before we start.

Who files the annual compliance audit return?

Ultra-High and Extra-High Level organisations file every year by 31 March. Every filing must carry a verification statement from a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO). We prepare the return and evidence with you and file it through a licensed DPCO.

What happens if we do not register?

The NDPA allows the Commission to impose penalties of up to the higher of ₦10 million or 2% of annual gross revenue for data controllers and processors of major importance. Registering and filing on time is far cheaper.

Can you help my firm become a DPCO?

Yes. We check your eligibility against the NDPC requirements, organise your documents and staff qualifications, and prepare the application on the portal. The NDPC fees are ₦50,000 to apply, ₦3,500,000 for the one-year licence once provisionally approved, and ₦1,500,000 a year to renew. Licensing decisions are made by the NDPC.
