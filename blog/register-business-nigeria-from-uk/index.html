Can I Register a Business in Nigeria While Living in the UK?

Yes — CAC registration is done online, so Nigerians in the UK can register a business name or company without travelling home. Here's what you need, what has to be in Nigeria, and how the process works remotely.

Yes. You can register a business name or a limited company in Nigeria while living in the UK, and you don't need to travel home to do it. The Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) runs registration through an online portal, so the filing itself happens the same way whether you're in Lagos or London.

What trips people up isn't distance. It's knowing which parts of the registration genuinely have to be Nigerian, which documents count as valid ID, and what changes if you or a co-owner hold only a British passport.

The one thing that must be in Nigeria is the business address. Your own home address can be in the UK. Everything else — your details, your ID, your signature — can be provided from abroad.

Business name or limited company?

The choice works the same way from abroad as it does at home, but a few points matter more for someone living in the UK:

Business nameLimited company
Who owns itYou personally (or partners)The company, owned by its shareholders
Your personal liabilityUnlimited — business debts are your debtsLimited to your shares
Banks, contracts, investorsAccepted for small tradingUsually preferred, and often required
Running it from abroadPossible, but everything is in your nameEasier to appoint a manager or director in Nigeria

If you plan to hire staff, sign larger contracts, or eventually hand day-to-day running to someone in Nigeria, a limited company is usually the better fit. Our guide to business name vs limited company goes through the trade-offs in detail.

What you need to provide

For each owner (proprietor, director or shareholder):

  • Full name, date of birth, nationality, occupation and phone number, exactly as they appear on your ID.
  • A valid means of identification. An international passport works. Nigerian citizens can also use their NIN. A UK driving licence may not be accepted, so use your passport.
  • Your residential address, which can be your UK address.
  • A signature, provided as a clear image (more on this in our guide to signing CAC documents from abroad).
  • A passport photograph in digital form.

For the business itself:

  • Two or three proposed names, in order of preference, in case the first is rejected.
  • The nature of the business — what you actually intend to do.
  • A business address in Nigeria (see do I need a Nigerian address?).
  • For a company, the share capital and how shares are split between shareholders.

If you hold only a British passport

Many Nigerians in the UK hold both passports, and registering as a Nigerian citizen is the simplest route. If you — or any co-owner — will register a company using only a non-Nigerian passport, the company is treated as having foreign participation, and extra rules apply:

  • Foreign shareholders register with a passport rather than a Nigerian ID.
  • A company with any foreign shareholder must have a minimum share capital of ₦100 million. That's far higher than a typical all-Nigerian company, and government fees rise with share capital, so our standard company package doesn't apply — we quote these registrations individually.
  • The company may also need to register with the Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission (NIPC) after incorporation, and other permits can apply depending on the business.

If you have dual nationality, it is usually worth registering as a Nigerian citizen with your Nigerian passport or NIN.

After registration: the parts people forget

  • Tax ID (TIN). Your registration generates a tax identification number for the business. Keep a copy.
  • Bank account. Opening a corporate account is where being abroad matters most. Some banks complete KYC remotely; others ask for documents to be certified, or for a director to appear in person. Ask your chosen bank early.
  • Annual returns. Every registered business files an annual return with CAC, whether or not it traded. Missing them leads to penalties — see our guide to CAC annual returns.
  • UK tax. If you're UK tax resident, income you receive from a Nigerian business may also need to be reported in the UK. Take UK tax advice on your own situation.

Do I have to travel to Nigeria at any point?

Not for CAC registration. The whole filing is online and your documents and signature can be sent from the UK. Some banks may ask to see a director in person when you open a corporate account, so check with the bank before you plan travel.

Can I use my UK address on the registration?

Yes, as your personal residential address. The business's own address must be in Nigeria.

Can a family member in Nigeria be a director or partner?

Yes. Many diaspora founders appoint a trusted relative or manager in Nigeria as a co-director. They'll need to provide their own ID and signature, and they share the responsibilities that come with the role.

How do I pay from the UK?

We send an itemised quote first and share payment details once you're happy with it. No payment is needed before you've seen the full cost.

The short version

Living in the UK is not a barrier to owning a registered Nigerian business. Prepare valid ID, a Nigerian business address and a clear signature, decide between a business name and a company, and the registration itself can be done entirely online — usually within one to three days.
