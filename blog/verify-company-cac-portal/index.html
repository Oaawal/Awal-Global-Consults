How to Check a Company Is Real: Verifying a Business on the CAC Portal

How to verify a Nigerian business before you pay, partner or sign: using CAC's public search, what the RC or BN number and status tell you, ordering an official status report, and the red flags that point to a fake.

CD Compliance desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

  • Step 2: Look deeper when the stakes are high
  • Step 3: Match the paperwork to the person
  • Red flags
  • The short version

Fake companies, cloned names and forged certificates are behind a lot of business fraud in Nigeria. The good news is that the first check takes minutes: the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) runs a public search of registered businesses.

Do this check before you pay a deposit, sign a contract or hand over goods on credit, not after something has gone wrong.

Step 1: Search the CAC register

Search CAC's public register by the business name or registration number:

  • Companies have an RC number.
  • Business names have a BN number.
  • Incorporated trustees have an IT number.

Check that:

  • The exact name exists: not just something similar.
  • The number matches the one on their certificate, letterhead or invoice.
  • The status is active. An inactive or struck-off status is a warning sign. See what happens when companies stop filing.
  • The registered address makes sense for the business.

Step 2: Look deeper when the stakes are high

For a significant contract, investment or credit, the public search isn't enough. Order an official status report or certified true copies of the company's documents, which show:

  • Directors and shareholders: are the people you're dealing with actually involved?
  • Share capital: does it fit the size of the deal?
  • Registered address and company secretary.
  • The company's filing history: are annual returns up to date?

Step 3: Match the paperwork to the person

  • Does the person you're dealing with appear as a director, or have a written authority from the company?
  • Is the bank account you're paying into in the company's exact registered name? Payments to personal accounts or differently named accounts are a classic fraud signal. See how businesses get scammed out of payments.
  • Do the TIN and other registrations match the same name?

Red flags

Red flagWhy it matters
Name not found, or only a similar name existsThe business may not exist, or is borrowing another firm's reputation
RC number belongs to a different companyCloned or forged documents
Status is inactive or struck offThe company may not be allowed to trade
Pressure to pay into a personal accountCommon in payment fraud
The "director" isn't on the CAC recordThey may have no authority to bind the company
Brand-new company offering an unusually large dealNot always fraud, but check harder

Is the CAC public search free?

The basic public search is available online. Official status reports and certified true copies are paid services.

Can a CAC certificate be forged?

Yes, which is why you should check the register yourself rather than rely on a certificate you've been sent.

Can I verify a foreign company the same way?

Foreign companies are registered in their own country's register. If a foreign company operates in Nigeria, it should also be registered locally.

The short version

Search CAC's public register, check the name, number and status, and for anything significant order an official report and match the directors and bank account to the company. Five minutes of checking can save a fortune.
