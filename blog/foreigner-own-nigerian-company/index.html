Can a Foreigner Own 100% of a Nigerian Company?

Yes, foreigners can generally own all of a Nigerian company, but with a ₦100m minimum share capital, NIPC registration, and sector exceptions. Here's what applies and in what order.

BD Business desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • What applies to a company with foreign shareholders
  • Where full foreign ownership isn't possible
  • The order things happen in
  • Foreigner or Nigerian abroad?
  • The short version

In most sectors, yes: a foreign individual or company can own 100% of the shares of a Nigerian limited company. Nigeria's investment law allows full foreign ownership outside a short list of restricted activities.

But "allowed" isn't the same as "simple". A company with any foreign shareholder has extra requirements from the moment it is registered.

What applies to a company with foreign shareholders

₦100 millionThe minimum share capital for a company with any foreign participation, whether the foreign shareholding is 1% or 100%.
  • Minimum share capital of ₦100 million. Government fees rise with share capital, so these registrations cost more than a standard all-Nigerian company and are quoted individually.
  • Registration with the Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission (NIPC) after incorporation, as a company with foreign participation.
  • Bringing in the capital properly. When foreign investment is brought into Nigeria through a bank, investors usually obtain a Certificate of Capital Importation (CCI) through that bank. Keep it: it supports the later repatriation of dividends and capital.
  • Permits for foreign staff. If the company will employ foreign nationals, it will need the relevant immigration approvals, such as an expatriate quota. See CERPAC and expatriate quota.
  • Sector licences. Some businesses need a licence or approval from a sector regulator before they can operate, whoever owns them.

Where full foreign ownership isn't possible

A small number of activities are closed to foreign investors, including those related to arms and ammunition, narcotic drugs and military or paramilitary items. Some sectors also have their own ownership rules or local-content requirements, for example in oil and gas, shipping and some communications businesses. If your business is in a regulated sector, check its rules before registering.

The order things happen in

  1. Choose the structure and share capital (₦100m minimum), and confirm the business isn't restricted.
  2. Register the company with CAC: the foreign shareholder registers with their passport.
  3. Register with NIPC.
  4. Open a bank account and bring in capital, obtaining a CCI where applicable.
  5. Get sector licences and staff permits the business needs.

Foreigner or Nigerian abroad?

If you're a Nigerian living abroad with dual citizenship, registering with your Nigerian passport avoids the foreign-participation rules entirely. See our guides for founders in the UK, US and Canada.

Does the ₦100m have to be paid in at once?

The company must be registered with at least ₦100m share capital. How and when the capital is paid in can be structured; we explain the options when we prepare your quote.

Can a foreigner register a business name instead?

Foreign investors usually use a limited company, which is the structure Nigeria's foreign-investment registrations are built around. Talk to us about your situation.

Does a Nigerian co-shareholder remove the requirement?

No. Any foreign shareholding makes it a company with foreign participation.

The short version

Foreign investors can generally own all of a Nigerian company, but plan for the ₦100m minimum share capital, NIPC registration, a Certificate of Capital Importation when money comes in, and any sector or staffing approvals.
