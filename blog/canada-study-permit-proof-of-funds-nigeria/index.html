Canada Study Permit Proof of Funds: What Nigerian Applicants Need to Show

How much money Nigerian students need to show for a Canadian study permit — tuition, the living-cost requirement that rose from 1 September 2026, travel — and which documents IRCC accepts, plus the mistakes that lead to refusals.

ID Immigration desk · Awal Global Consults 31 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • How much you need
  • Documents IRCC accepts
  • Common reasons Nigerian applications are refused
  • Comparing Canada with the UK and US
  • The short version

To get a Canadian study permit, you must show Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) that you can pay for three things: your first year's tuition, your living costs, and your travel. For Nigerian applicants, financial documents are one of the most common reasons for refusal.

How much you need

CAD 23,448IRCC's first-year living-cost amount for one person applying on or after 1 September 2026, anywhere in Canada except Quebec (up from CAD 22,895). Tuition and travel are on top.
CostWhat to show
First-year tuitionThe amount on your letter of acceptance
Living costsIRCC's current figure for one person, plus more for each family member coming with you
TravelEnough for your journey to Canada

Quebec sets its own, higher figures. Always check IRCC's own page for the amount in force on the date you submit — the requirement is assessed on the date your application is complete.

Documents IRCC accepts

  • Bank statements for the past six months, including the month you apply or the month before, showing where the money came from.
  • A Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank.
  • Proof of tuition already paid to the school.
  • A scholarship or funding letter.
  • Proof of a student or education loan from a financial institution.
  • If a sponsor pays: their bank statements, proof of income, and a letter of support stating their occupation, relationship to you and the amount of support, with proof of the relationship and their ID.

Large, unexplained deposits shortly before you apply are a major red flag. Officers are now expected to assess where the funds came from in every application, so be ready to explain the source of any significant deposit.

Common reasons Nigerian applications are refused

  • Funds that appear suddenly without an explanation of their source.
  • Sponsors whose income doesn't support the amount in their account.
  • Statements that can't be verified, or that look altered.
  • Not covering the full amount — tuition, living costs and travel together.
  • Using an outdated figure after the requirement changed.
  • Weak study plan — funds are only part of the decision. See why student visas get refused.

Comparing Canada with the UK and US

Each country assesses funds differently. See UK vs Canada vs US student visas and UK student visa financial proof.

Can my parents or a relative sponsor me?

Yes. Show their bank statements, proof of income and a letter explaining the support. Officers look at whether their income realistically supports the funds.

Is a GIC required for Nigerians?

Whether a GIC is required depends on the application route. Many applicants use one because it gives clear, verifiable proof of living funds.

Do I need to show funds for every year of my course?

IRCC's requirement focuses on the first year, but officers may consider whether you can fund the whole programme.

The short version

Show first-year tuition, IRCC's living-cost figure (CAD 23,448 for one person from 1 September 2026, outside Quebec) and travel — with a clear, verifiable history of where the money came from. Check IRCC's page on the day you apply.
