Signing CAC Documents from Abroad: E-Signatures, Notarisation and Power of Attorney

How Nigerians abroad sign CAC registration documents without travelling home, when a scanned signature is enough, when notarisation applies, and when you need a power of attorney.

Reviewed by Abdullateef Olamilekan Abdulwaheed, GRC Consultant, Founder of Awal Global Consults

If you're registering a Nigerian business from abroad, the signing step sounds harder than it is. CAC registration is filed online, and for the registration itself a clear image of your signature is usually all that's needed. The more formal tools, notarisation and powers of attorney, matter later, when banks, property or other people acting for you get involved.

1. Signing for CAC registration

During registration, each proprietor, director and shareholder provides a specimen signature: an image of your normal signature that is attached to the electronic filing.

To get it right:

  • Sign in black or dark blue ink on plain white paper.
  • Take a clear, well-lit photo or scan, cropped closely around the signature.
  • Use the same signature you use on your ID and bank documents. A signature that looks different from your passport can trigger questions later.
  • Send it the same way as your other documents: through our secure online application, by email, or on WhatsApp.

A blurred photo, a signature on lined paper, or a signature drawn with a finger on a phone screen are the most common reasons we ask clients to re-send. Thirty seconds with a pen and good light avoids the delay.

2. When notarisation comes in

Notarisation is when a notary public confirms that you signed a document, usually after checking your ID. It isn't normally needed for standard CAC registration, but you may be asked for notarised or certified documents by:

  • Banks, when opening a corporate account for a business whose directors live abroad.
  • Other institutions (landlords, regulators or business partners) who want certainty about who signed.

Documents signed abroad for use in Nigeria are commonly notarised where you live and then authenticated by the Nigerian embassy or high commission in that country. Requirements differ between institutions, so ask the one receiving the document exactly what it needs before you book a notary.

3. When you need a power of attorney

A power of attorney (PoA) authorises someone in Nigeria to act for you. It's useful when a step genuinely needs someone physically present or signing on your behalf, for example:

  • dealing with a bank in person,
  • handling property or a lease for the business,
  • appearing at an office where your attendance is required.

A PoA should state exactly what the person may do, and for how long. A broad, open-ended PoA gives someone a great deal of power over your affairs, so limit it to what is needed. A PoA signed abroad will usually need to be notarised and authenticated, as above, before it is relied on in Nigeria.

A power of attorney is a tool for specific tasks, not a substitute for appointing a director. If someone will run the business day to day, it's usually cleaner to make them a director or manager with a defined role.

Which do you need?

SituationWhat usually works
Registering a business name or company with CACA clear scanned specimen signature
Filing changes with CAC later (address, directors, shares)Signatures from the relevant directors or shareholders, provided the same way
Opening a corporate bank accountDepends on the bank, sometimes remote KYC, sometimes certified or notarised documents
Someone in Nigeria acting for you on a specific taskA limited power of attorney, notarised and authenticated
Someone in Nigeria running the businessAppoint them as a director or manager

Can I sign with a digital signature app?

For the CAC specimen signature, a scan or photo of your real handwritten signature is the safer choice, because it should match the signature on your ID. Some banks and partners accept e-signature platforms for their own documents: ask them directly.

Do all directors have to sign, even if they live in different countries?

Yes, each director and shareholder provides their own signature and ID. They can each send them from wherever they live.

How do I send my signature securely?

Our online application at awalglobal.com.ng/kyc stores documents privately and gives you a reference number to track your case. Email and WhatsApp also work if that's easier for you.

The short version

For CAC registration, sign on white paper, photograph it clearly and send it with your ID. Save notarisation and powers of attorney for the institutions and tasks that actually require them, and ask the receiving institution what it needs before you pay a notary.
