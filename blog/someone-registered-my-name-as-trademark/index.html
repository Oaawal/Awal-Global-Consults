Can Someone Else Register My Business Name as a Trademark?

Yes — CAC registration doesn't stop someone trademarking your name. Here's how it happens, your options at each stage (opposition, cancellation, passing off), the role of prior use and bad faith, and how to prevent it.

BD Brand desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • Your options depend on timing
  • The arguments that matter
  • What to do right now
  • The short version

Unfortunately, yes. CAC registration and trademark registration are separate systems. A business name or company name approved by CAC doesn't stop someone else applying to register the same name as a trademark with the Trademarks Registry — and if they succeed, they may try to stop you using it as a brand.

It happens more often than people think: a former partner, a competitor, a distributor, or someone who noticed your brand growing.

The best protection is simple: register your own trademark early. See business name vs trademark.

Your options depend on timing

StageWhat you can do
Their application has been published in the Trade Marks JournalFile an opposition within the two-month opposition window
Their mark has been registeredApply for rectification or cancellation — to the Registrar or the Federal High Court — for example on grounds of bad faith, your earlier use, or non-use
They are using the name against youRely on passing off if you have genuine goodwill in the name, alongside challenging their registration

The arguments that matter

  • Prior use. If you were using the name in trade before they applied, evidence of that earlier use is central. Keep invoices, adverts, packaging, social media history and customer records — with dates.
  • Bad faith. If they applied knowing the name was yours — for example a former partner or distributor — that can be grounds to challenge the registration.
  • Non-use. A registered mark that the owner hasn't genuinely used for a long period can be vulnerable to removal.
  • Your own registration. If you already have a CAC registration and evidence of trading, it supports your story — even though it isn't a trademark.
In trademark disputes, dated evidence wins. Keep proof of when you started using your name — you may need it years later.

What to do right now

  1. Search the trademark register for your name in your class — see how to do a trademark search.
  2. If no one has filed, file your own application immediately.
  3. If someone has filed, check the stage — the opposition window is short.
  4. Gather your evidence of use, organised by date.
  5. Don't ignore letters claiming you infringe their mark. Get advice before replying.

Can they force me to stop using my business name?

A registered trademark owner can take action against use of a confusingly similar mark. How strong their claim is depends on the facts — especially who used the name first and in what business. Get advice before you rebrand or respond.

How do I find out if someone has applied for my name?

Through a trademark search and by watching the Trade Marks Journal, where applications are published. We can check this for you.

Is it too late if their mark is already registered?

Not necessarily. Registered marks can be challenged in some circumstances, including bad faith and earlier use — but it's slower and harder than opposing at the publication stage.

The short version

Yes, someone can trademark your business name. If they've applied, oppose within the two-month window; if they've registered, consider cancellation; either way, gather dated evidence of your use — and register your own trademark before anyone else does.
