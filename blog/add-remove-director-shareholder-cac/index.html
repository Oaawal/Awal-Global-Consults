Adding or Removing a Director or Shareholder: The CAC Process

How Nigerian companies appoint or remove directors and bring in or buy out shareholders — the resolutions, share transfer documents, stamp duty, persons with significant control, and the CAC filings that make the change official.

BD Business desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • Directors and shareholders are different
  • Appointing a director
  • Removing a director
  • Bringing in a new shareholder
  • Persons with significant control
  • Shareholders abroad
  • The short version

Directors run a company; shareholders own it. Sometimes they're the same people, sometimes not — and when either changes, the change has to be decided properly inside the company and then filed with CAC. Until it's filed, CAC's records — which banks, partners and regulators rely on — still show the old position.

Directors and shareholders are different

DirectorsShareholders
RoleManage the companyOwn the company
How they changeAppointment or removal by resolutionTransfer, allotment or buy-back of shares
What's filed with CACNotice of change of directorsReturn of allotment or notice of share transfer, with updated members' details

Appointing a director

  1. Resolution appointing the director, as the company's articles require.
  2. The new director's consent to act, with their ID, address, contact details and signature.
  3. File the change with CAC promptly.
  4. Update the bank mandate if the new director will sign.

Removing a director

A director can resign by notice, or be removed by an ordinary resolution of the shareholders at a meeting. Removal has procedural requirements — including special notice of the resolution and giving the director a chance to be heard — so follow them carefully. Then file the change with CAC and update the bank mandate.

Most private companies need at least two directors; a small company can have one. Check that removing a director doesn't leave the company below its minimum.

Bringing in a new shareholder

There are two ways:

  • Transfer — an existing shareholder sells or gives some or all of their shares. This needs a share transfer instrument, payment of stamp duty, an update to the register of members, and notice to CAC.
  • Allotment — the company issues new shares to the incoming shareholder. If there aren't enough unissued shares, the share capital may need to be increased first by resolution. The allotment is then filed with CAC.

Persons with significant control

Companies must disclose their persons with significant control — people who own or control a significant stake (5% or more) or otherwise influence the company. When shareholdings change, check whether the PSC information needs updating too.

Shareholders abroad

Foreign or diaspora shareholders can sign transfer documents remotely — see signing documents from abroad. But if a non-Nigerian becomes a shareholder, the company becomes one with foreign participation, with a ₦100 million minimum share capital — see can a foreigner own a Nigerian company?

Is there a deadline for filing these changes?

Yes. Company law sets short deadlines for notifying CAC of changes to directors and shares, and late filings attract penalties. File as soon as the change is made.

Can a shareholder also be a director?

Yes. In many small companies the same people are both. The roles are still separate in law.

My business partner left years ago. Can we still update CAC?

Yes. Late changes can usually be filed with the documents showing when the change happened, plus any penalty. It's better done now than discovered during a bank review or tender.

The short version

Pass the right resolution, get the documents signed, pay stamp duty on share transfers, update the registers and PSC details, and file with CAC promptly. Your company's official record should always match who really owns and runs it.
