Buying land or a house back home is one of the most common goals for Nigerians abroad — and one of the most common ways people lose money. Almost every horror story has the same root cause: **paying before the documents were properly checked**.

When you're abroad, you can't walk the land, visit the registry or meet the seller's family. That makes independent verification essential, not optional.

::: callout
Never pay a deposit to "secure" a property before the title has been verified. A genuine seller with genuine documents won't object to a search.
:::

## The documents that matter

| Document | What it is | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)** | The government's grant of the right to occupy the land, usually for 99 years | That it is genuine, that it covers this exact land, and who it is registered to |
| **Survey plan** | A surveyor's map of the land's location and boundaries | That the coordinates match the land you're buying and the land is **not under government acquisition** |
| **Deed of Assignment** | The document that transfers the seller's interest to you | Prepared and reviewed by a lawyer, with the correct parties and land description |
| **Governor's Consent** | The State Governor's approval of a transfer of land under the Land Use Act | Whether the seller's own acquisition was perfected, and that yours will be after purchase |
| **Excision / gazette** (for family or community land) | Evidence that the land has been released from government acquisition | That the family selling has a valid right to sell |
| **Building approval** (for built property) | Planning permission for the building | That the structure was approved, so it isn't at risk of demolition |

## The searches that protect you

- **Land registry search** at the state land registry — confirms who holds the registered title and whether there are mortgages, caveats or other encumbrances.
- **Survey verification** — an independent surveyor confirms the land is where the plan says and isn't under government acquisition.
- **Litigation search** — checks whether the land or the seller is involved in court cases.
- **Physical inspection** — someone you trust visits the land to confirm it exists, who is in occupation, and whether there are disputes on the ground.

## Red flags that should stop a deal

- The seller resists a registry search or a lawyer's involvement.
- Documents are photocopies only, or the names don't match.
- The survey plan doesn't match the land, or the land is under acquisition.
- "Family land" sold without the agreement of the family head and principal members, or without excision.
- Pressure to pay quickly "before someone else buys it".
- A price far below the market for that area.

## Buying from abroad, step by step

1. **Instruct a lawyer in Nigeria** before you pay anything.
2. **Get copies of all title documents** from the seller.
3. **Run the searches** — registry, survey, litigation — and a physical inspection.
4. **Negotiate and document the agreement**, with payments made to accounts in the seller's verified name, never in cash through middlemen.
5. **Execute the Deed of Assignment.** If you can't sign in Nigeria, your lawyer will explain the options, which may include signing abroad and having documents notarised and authenticated, or appointing someone under a limited power of attorney. See [signing documents from abroad](/blog/signing-cac-documents-from-abroad/).
6. **Perfect the title** — apply for Governor's Consent and register the transfer, so the property is legally yours on record.

::: stat
Governor's Consent
The step most buyers skip. Without it, the transfer isn't perfected — and an unregistered buyer is in a weaker position if a dispute arises.
:::

## Buying through a company?

Some diaspora investors buy property through a Nigerian company. It can help with management and succession, but it has its own costs and tax consequences. Ask before deciding.

<p class="blog-faq-a">The seller's lawyer acts for the seller. You should have your own lawyer checking the documents and protecting your interest.</p>

<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">How long do the checks take?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Registry and survey checks usually take days to a couple of weeks, depending on the state. Governor's Consent after purchase takes considerably longer, and varies by state.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Is a C of O enough on its own?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">No. A C of O shows a grant to a particular person at a particular time. You still need to confirm it's genuine, matches the land, and that the seller is the current holder with nothing registered against the title.</p>
</div>

## The short version

From abroad, verify before you pay: title documents, registry and survey searches, a litigation check and an inspection — then perfect your title with Governor's Consent. A lawyer on the ground is the cheapest insurance you'll buy.
