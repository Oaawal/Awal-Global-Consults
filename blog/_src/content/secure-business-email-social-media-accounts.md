Your business email and social media accounts are where customers find you and where password resets for everything else are sent. If someone takes them over, they can lock you out, message your customers and reset your other accounts.

## The four settings that matter on every account

| Setting | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Two-step verification** | A stolen password alone isn't enough to get in |
| **Recovery email and phone** | You can get back in if something goes wrong. Make sure the business controls them |
| **Active sessions and devices** | Shows where you're logged in. Log out anything you don't recognise |
| **Who has access** | Remove former staff, agencies and anyone who no longer needs it |

::: callout
Use an **authenticator app** for two-step verification where you can. Codes sent by SMS are better than nothing, but SIM swaps can intercept them.
:::

## Business email (Gmail, Google Workspace, Outlook)

1. Turn on **two-step verification** in your account's security settings.
2. Save your **backup codes** somewhere safe and offline.
3. Check **forwarding rules and filters**. Attackers often add a hidden rule that forwards your emails to them.
4. Review **connected apps** and remove any you don't use.
5. If staff share one inbox, give each person **their own login** instead.

## Instagram and Facebook

1. Turn on **two-factor authentication** in Accounts Centre.
2. Make sure the **business page has at least two admins** you trust, so one compromised account can't lock you out.
3. Remove **old admins** and agencies.
4. Be careful with **"copyright violation" or "verification badge" messages**. They're a common way to steal logins.

## TikTok, X and others

Apply the same four settings. Wherever possible, link the account to a **business email**, not a personal one, so it stays with the business when people leave.

## If you're locked out

1. **Use the platform's official recovery route** only. Ignore "recovery agents" who message you; many are scammers.
2. **Warn your customers** on your other channels not to pay or click anything sent from the hacked account.
3. **Secure your email first**, because it controls password resets for everything else.
4. Once you're back in, **change passwords, turn on two-step verification and remove unknown devices and admins**.

## The short version

On every business account: turn on two-step verification, keep recovery details under the business's control, log out unknown devices and remove anyone who no longer needs access. Secure your email first, because it controls everything else.
