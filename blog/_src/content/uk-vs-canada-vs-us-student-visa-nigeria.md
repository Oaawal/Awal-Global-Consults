"Which country's student visa is easier to get?" is the wrong question, even though it's the one almost everyone asks first. The UK, Canada, and the US aren't ranked on a single difficulty scale. Each one is strict about a specific thing, and a profile that's strong against one country's requirements can be genuinely weak against another's.

Here's what actually differs, so the choice is based on your actual situation, not a rumour about which country is "easier this year."

## The United Kingdom: strict, but predictable

The UK's requirements are detailed but consistent: a fixed monthly maintenance figure, a defined documentation window, and clear rules about what counts as valid proof.

::: stat
£1,171 – £1,529
The current monthly maintenance requirement for UK study, outside London versus inside London, rising to a flat £14,130 threshold from 30 November 2026
:::

What the UK scrutinises hardest is **exact compliance with its financial documentation rules**: the 28-day holding period, the 31-day statement age limit, and unexplained deposits. Full details are in our [UK financial proof guide](/blog/uk-student-visa-financial-proof-nigeria/). An applicant who genuinely has the funds but doesn't document them to the letter of these rules is the classic UK refusal pattern. The upside is that because the rules are so specific, a well-prepared applicant can largely control their own outcome by simply following them precisely.

## Canada: currently the toughest odds, driven by financial scrutiny

::: stat
~25%
The approximate approval rate for Nigerian study permit applications in Canada between January and April 2026
:::

Canada's approval rate for Nigerian applicants has fallen sharply, and financial documentation has become both stricter and more heavily scrutinised: six months of bank statements are now recommended (up from four), a previously accepted proof-of-funds document (a convertible bank draft) was removed from the accepted list, and the baseline living-cost requirement sits around **$22,895 CAD** for a single student.

::: callout
Canada also places real weight on a well-written Letter of Explanation addressing ties to Nigeria and post-study intentions. A generic template letter is a common, avoidable weak point specifically for Canadian applications, more so than for the UK's more purely document-driven process.
:::

Right now, Canada is the destination where the financial bar and the approval odds are both moving in the harder direction simultaneously, which is worth weighing seriously against how strong an applicant's financial documentation genuinely is before choosing it as a primary target.

## The United States: less standardised, more interview-dependent

The US process differs structurally from the UK and Canada: alongside financial documentation, it includes an **in-person visa interview**, where a consular officer directly assesses ties to home country and genuine intent to return after study. There isn't a single published monthly maintenance figure the way the UK has; funding sufficiency is generally assessed against a specific institution's published cost of attendance instead.

::: quote
The US model puts more weight on how an applicant presents and answers questions in person, which rewards genuine preparation and a coherent, honest story, and punishes an applicant who's memorised answers without being able to speak naturally about their own plans.
:::

This makes the US a different kind of challenge: less about hitting an exact financial number to the day, more about a consistent, credible narrative held up under direct questioning.

## Putting the three side by side

| Factor | UK | Canada | US |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary scrutiny | Exact financial documentation rules | Financial evidence + ties-to-home letter | In-person interview + ties to home |
| Financial threshold | Fixed monthly figure (rising to £14,130 from Nov 2026) | ~$22,895 CAD baseline, rising documentation standard | Tied to institution's cost of attendance |
| Current difficulty trend | Stable, rule-based | Approval rates falling for Nigerian applicants | Interview outcome varies by individual |
| Where applicants most often fail | Documentation timing/format errors | Insufficient funds evidence, weak explanation letter | Weak or inconsistent interview answers |

## Choosing based on your actual profile, not the headline

- If your finances are genuinely solid and well-documented, and you can follow a detailed compliance checklist precisely, **the UK's rule-based system rewards that discipline.**
- If your financial documentation has any complexity, a sponsor involved, funds recently consolidated, income that's harder to paper-trail cleanly, **Canada's current heightened scrutiny is the riskiest environment for that specific weakness right now.**
- If you're a confident, coherent communicator with a genuinely clear post-study plan, but your financial documentation is less pristine on paper, **the US interview format gives you a channel to make that case directly that the other two don't offer.**
