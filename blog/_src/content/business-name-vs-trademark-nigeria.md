"I've registered my business name with CAC, so my brand is protected." It's one of the most expensive assumptions a Nigerian business can make.

CAC registration and trademark registration do **different jobs**. One lets you legally trade under a name. The other gives you the exclusive right to use a brand for particular goods or services. Many businesses need both.

## What each one actually gives you

| | CAC registration | Trademark registration |
|---|---|---|
| Registered with | Corporate Affairs Commission | Trademarks Registry (Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment) |
| What it does | Lets you carry on business under that name | Gives you exclusive rights to the mark for the goods or services in its class |
| What it stops | The same name being registered again **as a business or company name** | Others using the same or a confusingly similar mark **as a brand** in that class |
| Covers logos and slogans | No — names only | Yes — names, logos, slogans and more |
| Lets you take action | Limited | Yes — you can act against infringers |
| Duration | Ongoing, with annual returns | Seven years, renewable |

::: callout
A CAC certificate proves your business exists. It doesn't make the brand yours to the exclusion of everyone else.
:::

## Where the gap bites

- **Someone uses your name on their products.** If they haven't registered it with CAC, CAC registration gives you little to act on. A registered trademark does.
- **Someone registers your brand as a trademark first.** They may then challenge your use of it — even though you registered the business name earlier.
- **Your name clashes with an existing trademark.** CAC approval doesn't override trademark rights, and CAC has in the past directed companies to change names that conflict with registered trademarks.
- **You want to expand online.** Marketplaces and social platforms generally respond to complaints backed by registered trademarks.

## Do you need both?

| Your situation | What we usually suggest |
|---|---|
| Small local trading under your own name, no plans to build a brand | CAC registration is usually enough for now |
| You sell products or services under a distinctive brand name or logo | Register the business **and** trademark the brand |
| You plan to expand, franchise, attract investors or sell online | Trademark early — before you spend heavily on the brand |
| Your company name and product brand are different | Register the company with CAC and trademark the product brand |

::: quote
Trademark the brand before you print the packaging, not after. Rebranding is always more expensive than registering.
:::

## How to protect both

1. **Search first.** Check the trademark register as well as CAC before you settle on a name — see [how to do a trademark search](/blog/trademark-search-nigeria/).
2. **Register the business with CAC** so you can trade under the name.
3. **File the trademark** in the class that covers what you actually sell — see [trademark classes explained](/blog/trademark-classes-explained-nigeria/).
4. **Use the brand consistently**, and keep records of when and where you use it.

<div class="blog-faq">
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">I registered my business name years ago. Do I have any rights without a trademark?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Possibly. A business that has genuinely traded under a name can build up goodwill, and the law of passing off can protect it. But proving goodwill is slower and harder than relying on a registered trademark.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Does a trademark replace CAC registration?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">No. You still need CAC registration to carry on business under the name. The two work together.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Can I trademark my business name if it's a common word?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Very descriptive or generic words are harder to register. Adding a distinctive element — or registering a logo — often helps. A search will show what's realistic.</p>
</div>
</div>

## The short version

CAC lets you trade under a name; a trademark lets you own the brand. If your name is something customers recognise you by, protect it with both — and search before you invest in either.
