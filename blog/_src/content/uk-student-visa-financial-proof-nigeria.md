Insufficient or improperly documented funds is the single most common reason student visa applications from Nigeria get refused, more common than a weak academic profile or an unconvincing personal statement. The frustrating part is that most of these refusals aren't about not having enough money. They're about how the money was shown.

Here's what the UK actually requires from Nigerian applicants, and the specific mistakes that turn a genuinely fundable application into a refusal.

## The core financial requirement

A UK student visa applicant has to demonstrate funds covering two things: the **remaining tuition fees** owed to their institution, and a **maintenance (living cost) amount** for a set number of months.

::: stat
£1,171 / £1,529
The monthly maintenance amount required for students studying outside London versus inside London, for up to 9 months
:::

Students studying outside London need to show **£1,171 per month** for up to 9 months of maintenance. Students studying in London need to show **£1,529 per month** for the same period. This is on top of, not instead of, whatever tuition balance remains unpaid to your institution.

::: callout
A significant change is coming: from 30 November 2026, the required maintenance amount rises to £14,130, which works out to roughly ₦25 million at current exchange rates. If your travel or intake date falls on or after this change, plan around the new figure, not the older one still circulating in a lot of guidance online.
:::

## The rules about how the money has to be shown, not just how much

This is where applications actually fall apart, even when the applicant genuinely has the funds:

- **The balance has to be held for a minimum 28-day period**, and your bank statement can't be dated more than 31 days before your application. A statement that's slightly too old, or a balance that dipped below the required amount at any point during that 28-day window, doesn't satisfy the requirement, even if the balance is comfortably sufficient on the day you apply.
- **Large, unexplained deposits raise scrutiny rather than reassurance.** A lump sum that appears suddenly in an account shortly before applying, without a clear, documented source, can be read as borrowed or temporary funds rather than genuinely available money, which weakens rather than strengthens an application.
- **English proficiency documentation is a separate, parallel requirement.** Nigerian applicants generally need to demonstrate English proficiency at CEFR Level B2 or above, and this needs its own accepted form of evidence alongside, not instead of, the financial proof.

::: quote
An applicant with genuinely sufficient funds can still be refused for showing them the wrong way. The requirement isn't just "do you have the money." It's "can you prove, on the exact terms the rules specify, that you had it for the required period, from a source that holds up to scrutiny."
:::

## Sponsor-provided funds need their own paper trail

If a parent, relative, or sponsor is providing the funds rather than the applicant holding them personally, that relationship and the fund transfer need clear documentation: proof of the relationship to the sponsor, and evidence the sponsor has held the required amount for the same 28-day period the rules require of the applicant. A sponsor's funds showing up as an unexplained transfer into the applicant's own account, without that supporting trail, hits the exact same "unexplained deposit" problem described above.

## A realistic timeline for getting this right

::: stat
28 days
The minimum period your qualifying bank balance must be maintained before your statement is considered valid evidence
:::

Because of the 28-day holding requirement and the 31-day statement age limit, financial proof isn't something to assemble the week before you apply. Working backward from your intended application date, the funds need to already be in place, and held steadily, at least a month before you're ready to submit, with the statement pulled close enough to that submission date to still fall inside the 31-day window.
