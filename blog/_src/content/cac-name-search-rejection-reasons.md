Every registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) starts with a **name availability search**. If the name is rejected, nothing else can move, which is why it's worth understanding what CAC is looking for before you fall in love with a name.

## Why names get rejected

| Reason | Example | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| **Identical to an existing name** | "Sunrise Foods" when "Sunrise Foods" already exists | Add a distinctive word |
| **Too similar to an existing name** | "Sun-Rise Food" vs "Sunrise Foods": spelling, spacing or plurals don't make it different | Change the core word, not just the punctuation |
| **Restricted or sensitive words** | "Federal", "National", "Government", "Bank", "Insurance", "Cooperative" | Remove the word, or get the required approval or licence first |
| **Suggests a link with government or a regulator** | Names implying official status | Choose a neutral name |
| **Clashes with a registered trademark** | Using a well-known brand name | Check the trademark register too |
| **Offensive or misleading** | Names that are obscene, or describe a business you don't do | Make the name accurate and appropriate |
| **The structure word doesn't fit** | "Limited" in a business name, or a company name without "Limited" | Use the right ending for the structure |

::: callout
Adding "Global", "Ventures", "Nig." or "Services" to an existing name usually isn't enough to make it different. CAC looks at the distinctive part of the name.
:::

## How to choose a name that passes

- **Give two or three options**, in order of preference. If the first fails, the second can be used straight away.
- **Make the core word distinctive.** Invented words, a family name or an unusual combination pass far more often than common words like "Excellence" or "Divine".
- **Match the name to the business.** A name that suggests a different activity can be queried.
- **Avoid restricted words** unless you have the licence they imply.
- **Check beyond CAC.** Search the trademark register, social media handles and domain names, so you don't build a brand you can't protect. See [how to do a trademark search](/blog/trademark-search-nigeria/).

## Keeping a name you already use

If you already trade as a business name and want the same name for a new company, CAC's system may show it as taken: it can't tell you're the same owner. That's what the **consent route** is for. See [converting a business name to a limited company](/blog/convert-business-name-to-limited-company-nigeria/).

<div class="blog-faq">
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">How long is a name reserved?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">A reserved name is held for a limited period while you complete registration. If registration isn't completed in time, the name can become available to others again, so don't reserve and wait.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Does an approved name mean I own the brand?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">No. CAC approval lets you register and trade under the name; it doesn't give you trademark rights. See <a href="/blog/business-name-vs-trademark-nigeria/">business name vs trademark</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Can I use my own name?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Yes. Trading under your own true name doesn't require a business name registration, but adding words like "Ventures" or "Enterprises" turns it into a business name that must be registered. Banks also usually expect a registered business before opening a business account.</p>
</div>
</div>

## The short version

Pick a distinctive core word, avoid restricted and look-alike names, give CAC two or three options, and check trademarks and handles before you commit. A good name choice is the quickest way to a quick registration.
