"How much does it cost to register a trademark in Nigeria?" doesn't have a single honest answer, for a reason that surprises a lot of first-time applicants: **Nigeria doesn't allow multi-class trademark applications.** Every class you need protection in is its own separate filing, with its own separate fee. Get that structural fact wrong in your planning, and any number you were quoted for "a trademark" stops meaning much.

Here's what actually makes up the total, and why per-class filing changes how you should think about the cost.

## The one structural fact that changes everything

In many countries, a single trademark application can cover multiple classes of goods or services in one filing. Nigeria's system, run through the Nigerian Industrial Property Office (NIPO), doesn't work that way.

::: callout
If your brand needs protection across two or three classes, say, a product class and a related services class, that's two or three separate applications, each with its own government fee, its own journal publication, and its own timeline. There's no bundled multi-class discount built into the system.
:::

This is exactly why picking your class (or classes) correctly matters as much as it does, covered in our [guide to Nigeria's trademark classes](/blog/trademark-classes-explained-nigeria/). Filing in the wrong class doesn't just weaken your protection, it means you paid full price for a filing that doesn't actually cover your business.

## The components that make up the total, per class

| Component | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Clearance search | Confirming your mark isn't already registered or confusingly similar to an existing one, before you file |
| NIPO government filing fee | The statutory fee paid to the Nigerian Industrial Property Office per class filed |
| Trademark Journal publication fee | Publishing your accepted mark, opening the window for existing rights-holders to object |
| Professional/agent fee | Class identification, application preparation, filing, and follow-through until certificate issuance |

::: stat
7 years
How long a Nigerian trademark registration lasts before renewal — the same one-time filing cost buys protection for the full term, not an annual fee
:::

Government-set fees for the search, filing, and publication components are exactly that: government-set. They change periodically by gazette, the same way CAC's registration fees do, so treat any specific figure quoted (including elsewhere online) as a starting reference to confirm at the point of filing, not a locked-in number.

## Why two "trademark registration" quotes can look wildly different

This is the part that actually confuses people shopping around:

::: quote
A quote for "one class" and a quote for "your brand, properly protected" are often not the same quote, even when they use the same words.
:::

- **Number of classes.** A single-class quote and a two-class quote for the same brand can differ substantially, simply because you're paying the full fee stack twice, not because one provider is overcharging.
- **What "professional fee" actually includes.** A cheap quote that skips a proper clearance search, or skips class-identification advice, isn't a discount. It's a bet that your mark clears without a conflict and that you picked the right class unassisted, a bet that costs far more than the saving if it turns out wrong.
- **Renewal isn't included in registration.** The registration fee covers the initial 7-year term. Renewal, due before that term ends, is a separate, later cost, not something bundled into your original quote.

## What a properly itemized quote actually breaks down

Before comparing numbers, a trademark quote worth trusting should separate:

1. The clearance search fee
2. The NIPO government fee, per class
3. The Trademark Journal publication fee, per class
4. The professional/agent fee for preparation and filing
5. How many classes the quote actually covers

If a quote arrives as one flat number with no breakdown of these five things, that's worth asking about before you pay, not after.
