A cooperative society is registered by the **Director of Co-operatives** of the state where it operates (or by the Federal Director of Co-operatives, appointed by the President, for societies under federal supervision), under the **Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act** and the state's own cooperative law. It is **not** registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC).

Once registered, the society becomes a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal, and it can own property, enter contracts and sue or be sued in its own name (Co-operative Societies Act, s.6).

## What the law requires

Under the federal Act:

- **Members:** a primary society needs **at least 10 members**, each qualified under section 22 (generally aged 16 or over and within the society's area of operation; credit societies are exempt from the land rule). A secondary society needs at least 5 registered societies as members (s.3).
- **Purpose:** the society must be a limited liability society whose objects promote its **members'** socio-economic interests on cooperative principles (s.2).
- **Name:** must include the word **"Co-operative"** and end with **"Limited"**; "bank" or "banking" is not allowed except for a central financing society (s.3).
- **Bye-laws:** proposed bye-laws go with the application. The Director may change them to fit the Act (s.4) and must decide the application **within 60 days** (s.5). A refusal can be appealed to the Minister or Commissioner within 60 days.

States set their own detailed rules. **Lagos State**, under the Lagos State Cooperative Societies Law 2022, asks for:

- at least **20 foundation members**, aged **18 or over**, of good character and sound mind
- a **common bond** among members, such as a profession, trade or place of work
- a **verifiable secretariat address**

## Common types

Lagos lists, among others: Cooperative Multipurpose Society, Thrift and Credit, Consumer Thrift and Credit, Agricultural Multipurpose, Housing and Building, Transport, Artisan, Fishermen, Export, and Produce and Marketing societies. The type shapes the bye-laws and what the society may do.

## Steps in Lagos State

According to the Lagos State Department of Cooperative Services:

1. **Submit a formal application** to the Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Commerce, Cooperatives, Trade and Investment (MCCTI), Alausa, or through your local Cooperative Services Area Office.
2. **Hold and document an inaugural meeting**, with minutes.
3. **Pay the registration fees** set by the Ministry. Confirm the current amount with the Ministry before you pay.
4. **Officers attend an induction course** (president, secretary, treasurer) at the Lagos State Cooperative College.
5. **Buy the statutory record books** (cash book, ledgers and other registers).
6. **Undergo three months of monitoring and supervision.**
7. **Receive the Certificate of Registration and the approved bye-laws.**

Other states follow a similar pattern through their ministry or department of cooperatives. Ask the area office for its forms and current fees before you start.

## Documents to prepare

- An **application letter** stating the proposed name, type and area of operation
- **Minutes of the inaugural meeting**, with attendance and the officers elected
- **Proposed bye-laws** (the cooperative department often provides a model to adapt)
- A **list of foundation members**: names, addresses, occupations, signatures and their shares or contributions
- **IDs and passport photographs** of members and officers
- Proof of the **secretariat address**

::: callout
Choose the type and write the bye-laws carefully. They decide who can join, how much members may save and borrow, how surplus is shared and how disputes are settled. Changing them later needs the registrar's approval (Co-operative Societies Act, s.12).
:::

## Rules after registration

- **No loans to non-members**, except to another registered society with members' consent (s.30). A member may not join more than one society whose main purpose is lending to members without the society's consent (s.24).
- **One member, one vote** (s.25), and no member may hold more than a fifth of the share capital (s.27).
- **Surplus:** no dividend or bonus until the committee approves it, and at least a quarter of net profit goes into a reserve fund first (s.34).
- **Annual audit** by an approved auditor (s.36), plus annual accounts and returns to the cooperative department, regular meetings and an AGM (Lagos).
- **Inspection and dispute settlement** by the cooperative department, including mediation.

## The short version

Cooperatives are registered by the state (or federal) Director of Co-operatives, not CAC. You need at least 10 members under the federal Act (20 in Lagos), a name with "Co-operative" ending in "Limited", bye-laws and an inaugural meeting. In Lagos, officers take an induction course and the society is monitored for three months before the certificate. After that: member-only loans, one member one vote, a reserve fund before dividends, and an annual audit.

Sources: [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf), sections 2 to 6, 12, 22, 24, 25, 27, 30, 34 and 36; [Lagos State Department of Cooperative Services](https://mccti.lagosstate.gov.ng/departments/cooperatives) (checked 19 November 2026).
