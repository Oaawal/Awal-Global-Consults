Many Nigerians abroad register a business at home. See [registering from the UK](/blog/register-business-nigeria-from-uk/), [the US](/blog/register-nigerian-company-from-us/) or [Canada](/blog/register-nigerian-business-from-canada/). Registration can be done remotely. **Running** the business often can't, at least not entirely.

## What a local representative actually does

| Task | Example |
|---|---|
| Banking | Attending the bank, collecting cheque books or tokens, acting as a signatory |
| Documents | Collecting certificates, signing for deliveries, receiving official letters |
| Government offices | Following up with tax offices, licensing bodies or regulators |
| Property and premises | Managing the office, rent, staff and suppliers |
| Day-to-day operations | Supervising staff and reporting back |

You may not need someone for all of these. Decide exactly what you want them to do **before** you choose who.

## Who to choose

- **A family member or friend**: trusted and cheap, but informal arrangements cause the most disputes. Put it in writing anyway.
- **A business partner or co-director**: they share the risk and reward, but they also share control.
- **An employee or manager**: accountable to you, with a defined role.
- **A professional**: a lawyer, accountant or consultant for specific legal, tax or compliance tasks.

::: callout
Choose based on **what they're authorised to do**, not just who you trust. The person with the bank token or signing authority can bind your business.
:::

## How to give them authority

1. **Board resolution or written mandate**: for a company, a resolution saying exactly what they can do and for how long.
2. **Bank mandate**: set signing rules, for example two signatures above a set amount, or you approving payments online.
3. **Power of attorney**: for specific acts such as property or legal matters. Keep it limited in scope and time. See [signing CAC documents from abroad](/blog/signing-cac-documents-from-abroad/).
4. **Appointment as a director**: only if they'll really share in running the company. Directors have legal duties and wide powers. See [adding a director](/blog/add-remove-director-shareholder-cac/).

## Staying in control from abroad

- **Keep online banking access yourself**, with alerts on every transaction.
- **Limit what can be done without you**: payment limits, dual approval.
- **Keep copies of every certificate and filing**, and know your CAC login.
- **Check the CAC record regularly** to confirm nothing has changed. See [verifying a company](/blog/verify-company-cac-portal/).
- **Put a time limit on powers of attorney** and revoke them when they're no longer needed.

<p class="blog-faq-q">Does a Nigerian company need a director living in Nigeria?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">CAMA doesn't require a Nigeria-resident director, but banks, regulators and practicalities often make a local contact valuable.</p>

<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Is a power of attorney safer than making someone a director?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Often, because it can be limited to specific acts and a fixed period, while a director has broad powers. It depends on what the person needs to do.</p>
</div>

<p class="blog-faq-q">Can I use a registered address instead of a representative?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">A registered address handles correspondence, not decisions. See <a href="/blog/nigerian-address-cac-registration/">using a Nigerian address for CAC registration</a>.</p>
</div>

## The short version

Decide what you need done locally, give that person limited and written authority, and keep control of banking and records yourself. Trust is good; a clear mandate is better.
