**Almost certainly, yes.** The **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA)** applies to any business that collects or uses **personal data** — information about an identifiable person. That includes customer names, phone numbers, emails, delivery addresses, staff records, CCTV footage and WhatsApp contacts.

The NDPA requires you to tell people, clearly and before or when you collect their data, **what you collect, why, and what you do with it**. A privacy policy (or privacy notice) is how you do that.

::: callout
Size doesn't exempt you. The duty to be transparent applies whether you have 20 customers or 20,000. Registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission is a separate, additional step for businesses that process data on a larger scale — see [NDPA compliance](/blog/ndpa-compliance-nigerian-business/).
:::

## What your privacy policy must cover

| Section | What to say |
|---|---|
| Who you are | Business name, address and how to contact you about data |
| What you collect | The actual categories — names, phone numbers, IDs, payment details, CCTV |
| Why | The purposes — delivering orders, invoicing, marketing, security |
| Lawful basis | Consent, contract, legal obligation, or legitimate interests |
| Who you share it with | Delivery partners, payment providers, cloud services, regulators |
| Transfers abroad | If data is stored or processed outside Nigeria — for example on foreign cloud servers |
| How long you keep it | Real retention periods, not "as long as necessary" with nothing else |
| People's rights | Access, correction, deletion, objection, withdrawing consent, complaining to the NDPC |
| Cookies and tracking | If your website uses analytics or advertising cookies |

## Where it should appear

- **On your website**, linked from every page footer and from every form.
- **At the point of collection** — a short notice on forms, sign-up pages and WhatsApp catalogues, linking to the full policy.
- **For staff**, in employment documents.
- **In-store**, if you use CCTV — a visible sign.

## Why a copied template can hurt you

A template from another business — or another country — usually describes things you don't do and misses things you do. If something goes wrong, a policy that doesn't match your real practices shows the regulator exactly where the gap is. See [what to do after a data breach](/blog/data-breach-response-nigeria-ndpa/).

## A quick self-check

1. List every place you collect personal data — website, forms, WhatsApp, phone, in person.
2. Write down what you collect in each and why.
3. List every tool or company that sees it — payment providers, couriers, email services, cloud storage.
4. Decide how long you really need each type of data.
5. Make sure your policy says all of that, in plain language.

## The short version

If you collect personal data, the NDPA expects you to explain clearly what you collect, why, who sees it, how long you keep it and what rights people have. Write a policy that matches what you really do, and put it where people give you their data.
