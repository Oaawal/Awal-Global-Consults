Nigeria's **National Single Window (NSW)**, the government portal that already handles import permits, goes live for **exports on 14 December 2026**. The NSW project director, Tola Fakolade, confirmed the date at a stakeholder session in Abuja on 17 November, as reported by [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/nsw-processes-169000-permits-ahead-of-export-rollout/) and [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/national-single-window-harps-on-24-hour-export-cycle-as-phase-2-goes-live-dec-14/) on 18 November 2026.

Instead of visiting several agency portals and offices, exporters will submit regulatory documents once, through one portal that the relevant agencies can all see.

## What goes live on 14 December

The first batch of the export phase covers regulatory approvals and documentation from:

- **NEPC** (Nigerian Export Promotion Council): export registration and certification
- **SON** (Standards Organisation of Nigeria)
- **NAFDAC**
- **Federal Produce Inspection Service**
- **Nigerian Agricultural Quarantine Service**

NEPC's chief executive, Nonye Ayeni, said the council was connecting its electronic registration system to the NSW and expected that link within one or two weeks of the session.

## What comes later

A second batch, which Daily Trust reported for the first quarter of 2027 (March 2027, according to the NSW director), will bring in:

- **Central Bank of Nigeria** processes, including the **Nigerian Export Proceeds (NXP)** form
- **Nigeria Customs Service**
- relevant federal ministries

Until then, NXP and Customs steps carry on as they do today.

::: callout
The NSW does not replace your NEPC exporter's certificate or your NAFDAC and SON approvals. It changes **where** you apply and submit documents. You still need each registration your products require, and only a registered company can hold an NEPC exporter's certificate.
:::

## Why it matters

Fakolade said exports currently take about 10 to 12 days to clear and imports 10 to 21 days, and the long-term aim is 24 to 48 hours. Delays hit perishable produce hardest: goods can spoil at the terminal waiting for inspection, or arrive in poor condition and be rejected abroad.

The import phase, live since 27 March 2026, has processed more than 169,000 licences, certificates and permits, according to the NSW director.

## What to do before 14 December

1. **Check your NEPC registration is current.** Your exporter's certificate, company details and products should be correct before your records move across. See [NEPC export registration](/blog/nepc-export-licence-nigeria/).
2. **Gather product approvals in one place:** NAFDAC numbers, SON certificates, phytosanitary and inspection documents for each product.
3. **Make sure your company details match everywhere.** Names, RC number, TIN and addresses should be identical across CAC, NRS, NEPC and your bank, because a single portal will show any mismatch. See [verifying a company on the CAC portal](/blog/verify-company-cac-portal/).
4. **Attend a training session.** Nationwide two-day practical sessions for exporters began on 16 November at designated centres, according to Daily Trust.
5. **Keep your current channels for NXP and Customs** until the second batch goes live.

If you are still trading as a business name and want to export formally, register a company first. See [business name vs limited company](/blog/business-name-vs-limited-company-nigeria/).

## The short version

From 14 December 2026, exporters submit NEPC, SON, NAFDAC and inspection documents through Nigeria's National Single Window instead of separate agency portals. CBN (including NXP), Customs and ministries follow around March 2027. Check your NEPC registration and product approvals now, make sure your company details match everywhere, and attend a training session before the switch.
