The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (NTA 2025) and the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 apply from 2026. How they affect you depends first on **how your business is registered**.

## The comparison

| | Business name (sole trader) | Partnership (business name with partners) | Limited company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who is taxed | **You**, on the business's profit | **Each partner**, on their share of the profit | **The company**, on its own profit |
| Tax | Personal income tax | Personal income tax | Company income tax (CIT) |
| Rate | Progressive personal rates — first ₦800,000 of annual income tax-free, then 15% to 25% | Same as business name, per partner | **0%** for small companies; **30%** for others, plus the 4% development levy |
| Paid to | Your **state** tax authority | Each partner's **state** tax authority | The **Nigeria Revenue Service** (formerly FIRS) |
| Annual return | Your personal return | Each partner's personal return | The company's self-assessment return |

## Business names: you are the business

A business name has no separate legal identity, so it isn't taxed separately. The profit is **your income**, added to any other income you have, and taxed at personal income tax rates by the state where you live. See [the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 compliance guide](/blog/nigeria-tax-act-2025-compliance-guide/).

## Partnerships: each partner pays on their share

A partnership registered as a business name works the same way, split between the partners. Each partner pays personal income tax on **their share of the profit**, according to the partnership agreement. Keep proper partnership accounts so each partner's share is clear.

## Limited companies: the company pays

A company is taxed on its own profits, separately from its owners.

::: stat
0%
Company income tax for a **small company** — annual turnover of ₦100 million or less **and** fixed assets under ₦250 million, excluding professional services firms.
:::

- Small companies are also exempt from capital gains tax and the 4% development levy — see [the small company tax exemption](/blog/small-company-tax-exemption-nigeria-tax-act-2025/).
- Other companies pay **30% CIT** plus the **4% development levy** on assessable profits.
- Very large companies and multinational groups face a 15% minimum effective tax rate.
- What you pay yourself as **salary** is taxed as your personal income through PAYE, and **dividends** you receive can be taxed too.

## Rules that apply to every structure

| Rule | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **TIN** | Every business and every taxable individual — see [TIN retrieval](/blog/tin-retrieval-nigeria-2026/) |
| **VAT** | Businesses with turnover above **₦100 million** must register, charge 7.5% and file monthly — see [VAT for small businesses](/blog/vat-registration-small-business-nigeria/) |
| **PAYE** | Any business with employees must deduct and remit tax on salaries |
| **Withholding tax** | Qualifying payments to suppliers and contractors |
| **Filing even with no tax due** | A nil return is required by law. Not filing attracts ₦100,000 for the first month and ₦50,000 for each month after — see [no profit, still file?](/blog/no-profit-still-file-tax-returns-nigeria/) |

::: callout
The cheapest structure to register isn't always the cheapest to run. A growing business name can pay more personal income tax at higher bands than a small company would pay — while a professional services company can't use the 0% rate at all. Look at your expected profit, not just registration cost.
:::

## Which structure is better for tax?

- **Small, non-professional business with modest profit:** a small company can pay 0% CIT, but you'll still pay personal tax on what you take out as salary.
- **Professional services (consulting, law, accounting and similar):** no small-company exemption — compare 30% CIT plus levy with your personal rates.
- **Just starting and testing an idea:** a business name is simpler and cheaper to run — see [business name vs limited company](/blog/business-name-vs-limited-company-nigeria/).

Get advice from an accountant on your actual figures before choosing or changing structure.

## The short version

Business names and partnerships are taxed through their owners at personal income tax rates, paid to the state. Companies pay company income tax — 0% for small companies (₦100m turnover, under ₦250m fixed assets, not professional services) and 30% plus a 4% levy for others. Everyone must register for tax and file, even when nothing is due.
