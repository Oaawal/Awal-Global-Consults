Many businesses register a company, get busy, and forget the annual returns. Years later, a bank asks for a status report, a tender needs a CAC search, or the company appears on a striking-off list — and the backlog suddenly matters.

The good news: **if your company is still on the register, it can almost always be brought back into good standing** by filing what's outstanding. If it has already been struck off, there's still a route back, but it's slower and goes through the court.

## What happens when you stop filing

1. **Penalties build up** for each year that isn't filed.
2. **Your status shows as inactive**, which banks and partners will notice.
3. **CAC can strike the company off the register.** CAC has run large striking-off exercises against companies that haven't filed — publishing lists and giving a short window to file before removal.

::: callout
A struck-off company **cannot lawfully carry on business**. Its directors and members also stay liable for its debts and obligations as if it hadn't been struck off.
:::

## If your company is still on the register

1. **Check the company's status** and which years are outstanding.
2. **Prepare the missing returns** for every year, including the company's details for each period.
3. **Pay the filing fees and penalties** for the outstanding years.
4. **Update anything that has changed** — directors, shareholders, address — so the record is accurate. See [changes after CAC registration](/blog/change-business-name-address-partners-cac/).
5. **Complete any beneficial ownership (PSC) disclosures** that are outstanding.
6. **Confirm the status** shows active again.

Don't wait for a striking-off notice. Once a company's name appears on a published list, the window to file is short.

## If your company has already been struck off

CAC restores a struck-off company to the register **only on the order of the Federal High Court**. The company, or a member or creditor, applies to the court, usually showing that the company was carrying on business when it was struck off or that it's otherwise just to restore it. There is a time limit for applying, so act as soon as you find out.

Once the court orders restoration, the company files what's outstanding and the name returns to the register.

::: quote
Filing a late return costs a penalty. Restoring a struck-off company costs a court application. The difference is a reason to act this week, not next year.
:::

## Business names

Business names also file annual returns and can be removed for not filing. The same rule applies: file the outstanding years as soon as possible. See [CAC annual returns: deadline and penalties](/blog/cac-annual-returns-deadline-penalty/).

## The short version

If your company is still on the register, file every outstanding return, pay the penalties and update its details — now. If it's been struck off, restoration needs a Federal High Court order, and there's a time limit, so don't delay.
