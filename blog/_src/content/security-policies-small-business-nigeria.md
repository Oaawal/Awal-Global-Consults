A security policy is simply a written rule your business follows. Small businesses need them for two reasons: they make staff behave consistently, and banks, clients and tender panels increasingly ask to see them.

## The six policies that matter

| Policy | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Password and account policy** | Strong unique passwords, a password manager, two-step verification, no sharing logins |
| **Acceptable use policy** | What business devices, email and internet can be used for |
| **Device policy** | Screen locks, updates, encryption, lost devices, personal phones used for work |
| **Remote work policy** | Working from home, public Wi-Fi, keeping documents secure |
| **Payment security procedure** | Confirming bank-detail changes, approval limits, second sign-off |
| **Incident response plan** | What to do in the first hours of a breach, who to call, when to notify the NDPC |

::: callout
Keep each policy to one or two pages, in plain language. A short policy staff actually read beats a long one nobody opens.
:::

## Where data protection fits in

If you hold customer or staff data, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 expects you to keep it secure and to respond properly to breaches, including notifying the NDPC within 72 hours where required. Your incident response plan and privacy notice are part of showing you take this seriously. See [responding to a data breach](/blog/data-breach-response-nigeria-ndpa/) and [does my business need a privacy policy?](/blog/privacy-policy-small-business-nigeria/)

## Making policies work

1. **Write them for your business**, not copied from a large company.
2. **Get staff to read and sign** each one, and keep the record.
3. **Train staff** on what the policies mean in practice. See [staff cybersecurity training](/blog/staff-cybersecurity-training-small-business/).
4. **Review them once a year**, or after any incident.
