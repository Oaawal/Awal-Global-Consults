Registering the wrong structure causes problems later: a bank questions the account, members dispute balances, or a regulator steps in. Incorporated trustees (registered by CAC) exist for a cause and may **never** pay members a share of income or property (CAMA 2020, s.838). A cooperative (registered by the state Director of Co-operatives) exists for its **members' economic benefit**. Use the checklist below to see which side your group is on.

## Red flags: you are running a cooperative

If **any** of these describe your group's main activity, it is cooperative business and belongs in a registered cooperative, not in incorporated trustees:

| Red flag | Why it points to a cooperative |
| --- | --- |
| Members save a fixed amount every month and can withdraw their savings | Member savings are a cooperative (thrift) activity |
| Members borrow against their savings or contributions | Lending to members is what thrift and credit cooperatives do |
| Interest is charged on loans and the income is shared with members | Sharing surplus with members is forbidden for incorporated trustees (s.838) |
| A dividend, bonus or "end of year sharing" is paid to members | Dividends and bonuses from surplus are cooperative features (Co-operative Societies Act s.34) |
| Members buy shares or units that they can get back | Member share capital is a cooperative feature |
| The group buys goods in bulk for members, or sells members' produce together | Consumer, marketing and agricultural cooperatives |
| The group buys land and allocates plots or houses to members | Housing cooperatives |
| Membership is tied to a workplace, trade or profession and the benefit is financial | The "common bond" of a cooperative |
| The constitution talks of "shareholders", "returns" or "profit sharing" | Incompatible with CAMA s.838 |

::: callout
A church, mosque, club or association can still do these things, but through a **separate registered cooperative** run by the members who want it, with its own books and account. See [can incorporated trustees run a cooperative?](/blog/can-incorporated-trustees-run-a-cooperative-nigeria/)
:::

## Green flags: incorporated trustees is right

- The money raised (dues, offerings, donations, grants) is spent on the cause: worship, welfare, education, development, sport, culture.
- Members pay dues but expect **nothing back** in money.
- Welfare help to members is given from the association's funds as support, not as a return on contributions.
- The group would give its property to a similar body if it ever closed, not divide it among members (CAMA s.850).

## Bigger red flags: even a cooperative is not enough

Some activities need more than a cooperative registration:

- **Taking deposits from the general public**, not just members. Accepting deposits from the public is banking or microfinance business, which needs a licence from the Central Bank of Nigeria. A cooperative's loans are limited to its members and other registered societies (Co-operative Societies Act s.30).
- **Promising fixed or high returns to people who "invest"**, or paying earlier members from later members' money. That is an investment scheme regulated by the Securities and Exchange Commission, and a Ponzi scheme is a crime. No registration with CAC or a cooperative department makes it lawful.
- **Rotating savings (ajo, esusu) run as a business** for the public with fees. Informal member-only rotating savings among people who know each other is common; offering it to the public as a service raises licensing questions.

If any of these apply, take advice before registering anything.

## Before you file: five questions

1. **Who benefits from the money?** The cause (incorporated trustees) or the members themselves (cooperative)?
2. **Will members ever get money back beyond what they saved?** If yes, cooperative.
3. **Is the group's bond a faith, cause or community**, or a **workplace, trade or shared economic need**?
4. **Will you take money from people who are not members?** If yes, stop and get advice.
5. **Do you need both?** Then plan two bodies from the start.

<p class="blog-faq-a">CAC registers incorporated trustees only for the purposes listed in CAMA s.823 and can require more information about the objects (s.825). Objects about savings, loans or profit sharing are likely to be queried.</p>

## The short version

Savings, member loans, interest shared with members, dividends, bulk buying or land for members: that is a cooperative. Dues spent on a cause with nothing returned: incorporated trustees. Taking deposits or "investments" from the public needs a licence and advice, whichever you register.

Sources: CAMA 2020 Part F, sections 823, 825, 838 and 850; [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf), sections 30 and 34.
