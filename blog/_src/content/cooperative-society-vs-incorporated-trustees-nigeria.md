Churches, mosques, clubs, alumni associations, staff welfare groups, market associations and savings groups all ask the same question: **how do we register?** In Nigeria the two most common answers are a **cooperative society** and **incorporated trustees**. They sound similar because both are run by members and neither is an ordinary business, but they are built for opposite purposes.

The short test: **does the group exist to benefit its own members financially, or to pursue a cause?**

- If members pool money so that **they themselves** save, borrow, buy, sell or share a surplus, that is a **cooperative**.
- If the group exists for a **religious, educational, cultural, social, sporting, development or charitable purpose**, and money only ever goes to that purpose, that is **incorporated trustees**.

## Side by side

| | Cooperative society | Incorporated trustees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act (Cap N98) and each state's cooperative law | Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020, Part F (sections 823 to 850) |
| Registered by | The Director of Co-operatives of the state (or the Federal Director for societies operating federally) | The Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) |
| Purpose | Promote the economic and social interests of **its members** on cooperative principles | Religious, educational, literary, scientific, social, development, cultural, sporting or charitable purposes |
| Money to members | Allowed: dividends and bonuses from the surplus, after at least a quarter goes to a reserve fund (Act, s.34) | **Forbidden**: no dividend, bonus or profit to any member, directly or indirectly (CAMA s.838) |
| Minimum people | At least 10 members under the federal Act (s.3); Lagos asks for at least 20 founding members | At least 2 trustees, appointed by the association |
| Name | Must include "Co-operative" and end with "Limited" | Must contain "Incorporated Trustees of" |
| Lending | Lends to members; no loans to non-members except other registered societies (s.30) | Not a lending body; lending to members to share returns conflicts with s.838 |
| Control | One member, one vote (s.25); no member may hold more than a fifth of the share capital (s.27) | Trustees act under the association's constitution and its council or governing body |
| Oversight | State cooperative department: inspection, audit, dispute mediation | CAC: annual returns with audited accounts; changes need CAC approval |
| When it ends | Assets follow the liquidation rules of the cooperative law | Leftover assets go to a body with similar objects, never to members (s.850) |

## Which groups usually need which

**Usually a cooperative:**
- Staff thrift and credit groups ("cooperative" at work), where members save monthly and borrow against savings
- Market, transport, artisan or farmer groups that buy inputs together, sell together or share profits
- Housing groups buying land to share among members
- Any group that pays members a share of a surplus at year end

**Usually incorporated trustees:**
- Churches, mosques, ministries and fellowships
- NGOs, foundations and charities
- Alumni associations, town unions and community development associations
- Social, sports and cultural clubs, professional and religious associations

Some groups do both: a church may run its ministry as incorporated trustees while its members form a separate staff or members' cooperative. That is allowed, but they are **two separate legal bodies** with separate books, accounts and registrations. See [can incorporated trustees run a cooperative?](/blog/can-incorporated-trustees-run-a-cooperative-nigeria/)

::: callout
The word "Co-operative" is protected. Only a registered cooperative society may trade under a name containing it without the Director's permission (Co-operative Societies Act, s.54). An association registered with CAC should not call itself a cooperative.
:::

## How registration differs

**Incorporated trustees** register online with CAC: name consent, a 28-day public notice in two newspapers, sworn trustee declarations and final approval, usually five to six weeks in our experience. See [how to register an NGO, church or association](/blog/register-ngo-church-incorporated-trustees-nigeria/).

**Cooperatives** register with the state ministry responsible for cooperatives. In Lagos that is the Department of Cooperative Services of the Ministry of Commerce, Cooperatives, Trade and Investment, which requires an inaugural meeting, an induction course for officers and three months of monitoring before the certificate. See [how to register a cooperative society](/blog/how-to-register-cooperative-society-nigeria/).

<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Can a cooperative also do charity?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Yes, within its bye-laws and the cooperative law (the federal Act allows an education fund from surplus). But a group whose main purpose is charity or worship should register as incorporated trustees.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Is a cooperative registered with CAC?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">No. Cooperatives are registered by the state (or federal) Director of Co-operatives, not CAC. Incorporated trustees, companies and business names are registered by CAC.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Can incorporated trustees pay salaries?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Yes, reasonable pay for staff and officers who actually do the work is allowed. What is forbidden is sharing income or property with members as profit, and members of the governing council generally cannot hold salaried posts (CAMA s.838).</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Which is cheaper to run?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Both need proper books, annual accounts and returns. Incorporated trustees file annual returns with audited accounts at CAC; cooperatives keep statutory books, hold an AGM and submit accounts to the state cooperative department.</p>
</div>
</div>

## The short version

A cooperative is for members who pool money to benefit themselves; incorporated trustees are for a cause, and members can never share in the money. They follow different laws (the Co-operative Societies Act vs CAMA Part F) and different regulators (the state Director of Co-operatives vs CAC). Pick by purpose, and if your group does both, register two separate bodies.
