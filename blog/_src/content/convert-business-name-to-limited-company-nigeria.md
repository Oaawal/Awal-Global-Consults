Many businesses start as a business name because it's quick and cheap, then outgrow it. A bank asks for a company. A big client wants to contract with a limited company. You want to bring in a partner as a shareholder, or protect your personal assets.

The good news: **you can move up to a limited company and keep your business name.** The thing to understand is that a business name and a company are different kinds of legal thing, so "converting" really means **creating a company and moving the business into it**.

## Why the two are different

A **business name** is a trading name registered to you as the proprietor. You and the business are legally the same person, so its debts are your debts.

A **limited company** is a separate legal person. It owns its own assets, signs its own contracts, and its shareholders' liability is limited to their shares.

Because of that difference, a business name can't simply be relabelled as a company. A new company is incorporated, and the business carries on through it.

::: callout
Becoming a company protects you from the company's debts **going forward**. Debts and obligations the business name took on before the move stay with you personally.
:::

## The two routes

| | Route 1: Upgrade with consent | Route 2: New company, then close the business name |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | You apply to CAC for consent to use your existing business name for a new company ("Your Brand Limited"), using your business name details | You register a new company — with the same name if it's available, or a new one — and then file to cease the business name |
| Keeps the same name | Yes — this is the main reason to use it | Only if the name is available as a company name |
| Typical requirements | Your business name certificate, up-to-date annual returns, a consent application, then normal company registration | Normal company registration, then a cessation filing for the business name |
| Best for | Established brands that want to be sure of keeping their name | When the name is free to register as a company, or you're happy with a new name |

CAC's exact requirements for the consent route can change, so we confirm the current documents and fees before starting. One requirement is consistent: **your business name's annual returns should be up to date** before you apply. If they're behind, that's the first thing to fix (see our guide to [CAC annual returns](/blog/cac-annual-returns-deadline-penalty/)).

## Choosing your company set-up

The move is a good moment to decide:

- **Shareholders and shares** — who owns what, especially if a partner is joining.
- **Directors** — who will run the company. One director is enough for a small company.
- **Share capital** — enough for your needs and your bank's expectations.

## What to update after the move

This is the part people underestimate. Once the company exists:

- **Tax.** The company has its own TIN and its own tax position — see [Nigeria Tax Act 2025: TIN, filing and penalties](/blog/nigeria-tax-act-2025-compliance-guide/).
- **Bank account.** Open a corporate account in the company's name, and move payments across.
- **Contracts and invoices.** New contracts should be signed by the company. Existing ones may need to be assigned or re-signed with the other party's agreement.
- **Licences and registrations** held under the business name — these generally need to be obtained again in the company's name.
- **Branding and paperwork.** Letterheads, website, invoices and email signatures should show the company name and RC number.
- **The business name itself.** Decide whether to keep it (it still needs annual returns) or file to cease it.

::: quote
The certificate is the easy part. The real switch happens when your bank account, contracts and invoices are all in the company's name.
:::

## The short version

You can't flip a business name into a company, but you can incorporate a company — usually keeping your name — and move the business into it. Get your annual returns up to date, choose your shareholders and directors carefully, and plan the bank, tax and contract switch-over from the start.
