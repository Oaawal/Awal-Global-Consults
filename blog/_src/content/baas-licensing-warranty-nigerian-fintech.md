If you're building a wallet, payments, savings or lending product in Nigeria, you'll probably work with a **banking-as-a-service (BaaS)** provider: a licensed bank or payments company whose accounts, rails or licence your product runs on. Before they switch you on, they'll send an agreement. One clause in it deserves more attention than founders usually give it: the **licensing warranty**.

## What the clause usually says

Wording varies, but a licensing warranty typically has you **promise** that:

- you hold **all the licences, approvals and registrations** needed for your business, **or**
- your business, as you run it, **doesn't need** a licence of its own, and
- you'll **tell the partner** if that changes, and
- you'll **indemnify** the partner (pay their losses) if the promise turns out to be untrue.

::: callout
A warranty is a statement of fact you are responsible for. If a regulator later decides your product needed a licence, the partner can rely on the clause to suspend you, terminate, and ask you to cover their costs.
:::

## Why it catches founders out

- **Founders assume the partner's licence covers them.** Sometimes it does, for part of what you do. Often it doesn't cover everything, especially once the product changes.
- **The product drifts.** You start as a simple collections tool and add stored balances, transfers or credit. Each change can move you into a different regulatory category.
- **The company isn't set up for it.** Your CAC objects (MEMART) may not include payments or financial technology at all. See [changes after CAC registration](/blog/change-business-name-address-partners-cac/).

## The categories to think about

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) licenses payment businesses in categories such as switching and processing, mobile money, and payment solution services, alongside banks and microfinance banks. Which category, if any, applies to you depends on one core question: **who holds the customer's money, and who moves it?** If your product holds or controls customer funds, you are in licensed territory unless a licensed partner genuinely does that for you.

## What to check before you sign

1. **Map your money flow.** Draw where the money sits at each step, and whose account it's in.
2. **Get the partner to confirm in writing** which parts of your product their licence covers, and which parts you're responsible for.
3. **Check your MEMART objects** cover what you actually do, and amend them if not.
4. **Read the warranty and indemnity together.** Look for a knowledge qualifier ("to the best of its knowledge"), a cap on liability, and a notice-and-cure period before termination.
5. **Check the other obligations** usually bundled in: KYC duties, data protection, transaction limits and reporting.
6. **Don't sign a warranty you can't stand behind.** Negotiate the wording, or change the product or structure first.

## When the answer is "you need your own licence"

That isn't the end. Options usually include restructuring the product so a licensed partner holds funds, partnering differently, or planning a licence application with the capital and governance it requires. See [which licences a new business needs](/blog/licences-new-business-nigeria/).

## The short version

A BaaS licensing warranty is a promise about your regulatory position that the partner can enforce later. Map your money flow, get the partner's coverage in writing, fix your MEMART objects, and negotiate the wording before you sign.
