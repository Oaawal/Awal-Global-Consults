Social clubs, sports clubs, alumni sets, old students' associations, town and village unions, professional associations, market associations, cultural groups and WhatsApp communities often ask whether they need to register. The honest answer: **not always**, but an unregistered group has real limits once money and property are involved.

## When an informal group is fine

A group can operate informally if it only meets, organises events and collects small dues that are spent quickly. But without registration the group is **not a legal person**:

- It cannot open a bank account in its own name; money ends up in an officer's personal account.
- It cannot own land or buildings; property sits in individuals' names.
- It cannot sue or be sued in its own name, sign contracts or receive most grants.
- If an officer dies, leaves or disappears with the money, members have little protection.

## Signs it is time to register

- You hold more than a few months of dues or project funds
- You are buying land, building a hall or receiving donated property
- You want a bank account in the group's name
- You are applying for grants or partnering with government, companies or donors
- Leadership is changing and you want the group to outlast its founders

## Your options

| Structure | Registered with | Best for | Key limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Incorporated trustees** | CAC (CAMA 2020 Part F) | Social, sporting, cultural, alumni, town development, professional and religious groups | Money goes only to the group's objects; nothing paid to members as profit (s.838) |
| **Company limited by guarantee** | CAC | Larger member-based non-profits that want a board and members rather than trustees | More formal governance; see [incorporated trustees vs CLG](/blog/incorporated-trustees-vs-company-limited-by-guarantee/) |
| **Cooperative society** | State Director of Co-operatives | Groups whose members save, borrow, buy or sell together for their own benefit | Must follow cooperative law; members only |
| **Limited company** | CAC | A club that runs a profit-making business (bar, restaurant, events company) | Profit-making; taxed as a company |

CAMA expressly allows registration as incorporated trustees for communities bound by **custom, kinship or nationality** and associations for **social, development, cultural or sporting** purposes (s.823). That covers most clubs, alumni sets and town unions.

::: callout
If your group's real activity is monthly savings, loans to members or sharing money at year end, incorporated trustees is the wrong structure. See [red flags: when your association should be a cooperative](/blog/signs-association-should-be-cooperative-nigeria/).
:::

## Notes for particular groups

- **Alumni and old students' associations:** register the national body as incorporated trustees; chapters and sets can operate as branches under its constitution rather than as separate registrations.
- **Town unions and community development associations:** incorporated trustees is the usual route for holding community land, halls and project funds. Some states also expect community development associations to register with the local government; ask your local government council.
- **Sports and social clubs:** a club that sells drinks or runs a bar may need state permits for that activity on top of its registration. A club running a commercial operation for profit should put it in a company.
- **Professional associations and market associations:** incorporated trustees suits standard-setting, welfare and advocacy. A traders' scheme to buy stock in bulk or lend to members is a cooperative.

## What you need to register as incorporated trustees

- A name, "The Incorporated Trustees of …"
- At least **two trustees** (three to seven is safer), appointed at a meeting with minutes showing attendance and votes (s.825)
- A **constitution** covering trustees, meetings, the governing body, and how dues and contributions are collected, spent, accounted for and audited (s.827)
- Trustees' IDs, photographs, signatures and sworn declarations

The full process and timeline (about five to six weeks, including a 28-day public notice) is in [how to register an NGO, church or association](/blog/register-ngo-church-incorporated-trustees-nigeria/). After registration, see [the CAMA 2020 rules for incorporated trustees](/blog/incorporated-trustees-rules-cama-2020/).

## The short version

An informal group is fine while it only meets and spends small dues. Once it holds money, owns property or needs a bank account, register: incorporated trustees for clubs, alumni sets, town unions and associations; a cooperative if members save, borrow or share money; a company for any profit-making business.

Sources: Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020, Part F, sections 823, 825, 827 and 838.
