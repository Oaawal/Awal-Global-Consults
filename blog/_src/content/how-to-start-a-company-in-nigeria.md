Starting a company in Nigeria isn't one big task. It's about ten smaller ones, and most problems come from doing them in the wrong order: registering before deciding who owns what, or opening a bank account before the CAC record is right. This roadmap puts every step in order, with a detailed guide for each.

::: callout
Steps 1 to 6 can often be done within a week. A limited company is usually approved within 48 to 72 hours once complete details are filed, if CAC raises no queries.
:::

## Step 1: Decide if you need a company at all

A **business name** is cheaper and simpler. A **limited company** gives you a separate legal identity, limited liability and an easier path to investors, big clients and banks. Read [business name vs limited company](/blog/business-name-vs-limited-company-nigeria/) before you choose. Non-profits use [incorporated trustees or a company limited by guarantee](/blog/incorporated-trustees-vs-company-limited-by-guarantee/) instead.

## Step 2: Decide who owns and runs it

- **Shareholders** own the company. One person can own all of it. See [single-member companies](/blog/single-member-company-nigeria/).
- **Directors** run it. A small company can have one director; others need at least two. See [who can be a director](/blog/who-can-be-director-nigerian-company/).
- A **foreign shareholder** changes the rules, including a ₦100 million minimum share capital. See [can a foreigner own a Nigerian company?](/blog/foreigner-own-nigerian-company/)

## Step 3: Choose your share capital

The legal minimum for a private company is ₦100,000, but the right figure depends on your bank, contracts and licences. Government fees rise with share capital. See [how much share capital to register](/blog/share-capital-new-company-nigeria/) and the official [CAC fees for 2026](/blog/cac-fees-2026-fee-table/).

## Step 4: Pick a name CAC will accept

Prepare two or three options. Avoid names that are identical or too similar to existing ones, or that use restricted words without approval. See [why CAC names get rejected](/blog/cac-name-search-rejection-reasons/).

## Step 5: Register with CAC

Gather each director's and shareholder's details, ID, photograph and signature, then file. Use the [company registration checklist](/blog/company-registration-document-checklist/). You can do it yourself on the portal or use an agent. See [CAC registration online](/blog/cac-registration-online-nigeria/) and [do I need a lawyer?](/blog/do-i-need-lawyer-register-company-nigeria/)

## Step 6: Get your TIN

Your company needs a Tax Identification Number for its bank account, invoices and tax filings. You'll need a recent utility bill for the business address. See [TIN retrieval](/blog/tin-retrieval-nigeria-2026/).

## Step 7: Open a corporate bank account

Banks will ask for your CAC documents, directors' IDs and BVNs, a board resolution and proof of address. See [opening a corporate bank account](/blog/open-corporate-bank-account-nigeria/).

## Step 8: Get any licences your sector needs

Food, drugs, cosmetics, exports, telecoms, finance and some other sectors need a regulator's licence before you trade. See [which licences you need](/blog/licences-new-business-nigeria/). Some businesses also need [SCUML registration](/blog/scuml-registration-nigeria/).

## Step 9: Protect your brand

Company registration doesn't stop others using your brand. A trademark does. See [business name vs trademark](/blog/business-name-vs-trademark-nigeria/).

## Step 10: Stay compliant every year

Diary your [compliance calendar](/blog/nigeria-business-compliance-calendar/): CAC annual returns, tax returns (even with no profit), PAYE and, above ₦100 million turnover, VAT. Small companies pay 0% company income tax but must still file. See [how each business type is taxed](/blog/business-name-partnership-company-tax-nigeria/).

## The short version

Decide the structure and who owns and runs it, choose share capital and a name, register with CAC, then get your TIN, bank account, licences and trademark. After that, keep up with annual returns and tax filings every year.
