Registering with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) gives your business a legal identity. For many businesses — a consultancy, an online shop selling general goods, a design studio — that plus tax registration is most of what's needed to start. But in **regulated sectors**, operating without the right licence can mean fines, closure or seized goods.

::: callout
Think of it in three layers: **everyone** (CAC, tax), **your sector** (a regulator's licence or product registration), and **your location** (state and local permits).
:::

## Layer 1: what almost every business needs

| Registration | Who issues it |
|---|---|
| Business name or company registration | CAC — see [business name vs limited company](/blog/business-name-vs-limited-company-nigeria/) |
| Tax Identification Number (TIN) | Tax authority — see [TIN retrieval](/blog/tin-retrieval-nigeria-2026/) |
| Employer registrations, once you hire | Pension, PAYE and social insurance schemes |
| Data protection compliance, if you handle personal data | See [does my business need a privacy policy?](/blog/privacy-policy-small-business-nigeria/) |

## Layer 2: sector licences and registrations

Common examples:

| If your business… | You may need… | Regulator |
|---|---|---|
| Makes, imports or sells packaged food, drinks, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices, chemicals or packaged water | Product registration and, for makers, premises inspection | **NAFDAC** (National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control) |
| Makes regulated products in Nigeria | Product certification under the **MANCAP** scheme | **SON** (Standards Organisation of Nigeria) |
| Imports regulated products | Product certificates under the **SONCAP** scheme | **SON** |
| Exports goods | Exporter registration (export licence) — see [NEPC export licence](/blog/nepc-export-licence-nigeria/) | **NEPC** (Nigerian Export Promotion Council) |
| Offers telecoms, internet or communications services | A licence for the relevant service category | **NCC** (Nigerian Communications Commission) |
| Runs a bank, microfinance bank, payment or money-transfer service | A banking or payments licence | **CBN** (Central Bank of Nigeria) |
| Offers digital lending to consumers | Registration as a digital lender | **FCCPC** (Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission) |
| Runs a courier or delivery business | A courier operator's licence | **NIPOST** courier regulatory department |
| Runs a pharmacy or sells medicines | Premises registration and a superintendent pharmacist | **PCN** (Pharmacy Council of Nigeria) |
| Runs a school | Approval to operate | State ministry of education |
| Runs a hospital, clinic or laboratory | Registration of the facility | State ministry of health, plus the relevant professional bodies |
| Operates in oil and gas | Upstream permits, or midstream and downstream licences | **NUPRC** (upstream) or **NMDPRA** (midstream and downstream) |
| Deals in real estate, cars, jewellery or other designated businesses | AML registration — see [SCUML for real estate agents, car dealers and jewellers](/blog/scuml-real-estate-car-dealers-jewellers-nigeria/) | **SCUML** |
| Is an NGO, church or association | AML registration — see [SCUML registration](/blog/scuml-registration-nigeria/) | **SCUML** |

Requirements, categories and fees change, so confirm each regulator's current rules before you apply — or ask us.

## Layer 3: state and local permits

States and local governments often require:

- **Business premises or trade permits**, renewed yearly.
- **Signage and advertising permits** for shop signs and billboards.
- **Environmental and waste permits** for some operations.
- **Building and fire safety approvals** for premises.

These differ from state to state, so check with the state and local government where you operate.

## How to work out what you need

1. **Describe exactly what you'll do** — what you sell, make, import or export, and where.
2. **Check whether a regulator covers that activity.** If the product goes into people's bodies, onto their skin, or into their money, a regulator almost certainly does.
3. **Check your state and local government's permits** for premises and signage.
4. **Make sure your CAC business objects cover the activity** — regulators check this.
5. **Diary renewal dates.** Many licences and permits are annual.

## The short version

CAC and tax registration cover most simple businesses. Regulated products and services need a sector licence, and premises often need state and local permits. Check before you trade, make sure your business objects match, and keep track of renewals.
