Fake payment requests, phishing links and "send me the code" messages all work by fooling a person. That's why a short, practical training session is one of the best-value security steps a small business can take.

## What a good session covers

| Topic | What staff should be able to do afterwards |
|---|---|
| **Phishing** | Spot fake emails, SMS and messages, and know not to click or reply |
| **Verification-code scams** | Never share a WhatsApp, bank or email code, whoever asks |
| **Payment fraud** | Confirm any change of bank details by phone, and recognise pressure tactics |
| **Passwords** | Use a password manager and two-step verification |
| **Devices** | Lock screens, install updates and report a lost device straight away |
| **Customer data** | Share it only with people who need it, and know what a data breach is |
| **Reporting** | Know exactly who to tell, and that reporting a mistake early is always right |

::: callout
The most important message for staff: **report a mistake straight away**. A clicked link reported in five minutes is a small problem. One hidden for a week can become a breach you have to report to the NDPC.
:::

## Make it practical

- **Use real local examples**: fake bank alerts, "the CEO needs this paid now" messages, WhatsApp takeover attempts.
- **Keep it short**: one to two hours works better than a full day.
- **Give a one-page reference** staff can keep at their desk.
- **Test understanding** with a few real-looking examples at the end.

## How often to train

- **When someone joins**, as part of their first week.
- **Once a year for everyone**, with a refresher on new scams.
- **After any incident**, so the whole team learns from it.

## Why it matters for compliance

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, businesses must keep personal data secure. Training staff is a basic part of showing you took reasonable steps. Keep a record of who attended and when. See [NDPA compliance](/blog/ndpa-compliance-nigerian-business/).

## The short version

Train staff to spot phishing, never share verification codes, confirm bank-detail changes by phone, use strong passwords and report mistakes fast. Keep it short and practical, repeat it yearly, and keep a record.
