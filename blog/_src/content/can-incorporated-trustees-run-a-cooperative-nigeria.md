**No.** An association registered as incorporated trustees with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) cannot convert into a cooperative society, and it should not run cooperative-style activities such as member savings with interest or sharing a surplus among members. The two are created under different laws, by different regulators, for opposite purposes.

What you **can** do is set up a separate cooperative alongside the association. Details below.

## Why the law says no

**1. Incorporated trustees may never share money with members.** CAMA 2020, section 838 says the income and property of incorporated trustees must be applied **solely** to the objects in the constitution, and **no portion may be paid or transferred, directly or indirectly, by way of dividend, bonus or profit to any member**. A cooperative exists precisely to return benefits to its members: the Co-operative Societies Act describes a bonus as a share of the surplus divided among members according to the business they did with the society, and section 34 sets out how dividends and bonuses are paid from net surplus.

**2. The purposes are different.** Incorporated trustees are for religious, educational, literary, scientific, social, development, cultural, sporting or charitable purposes (CAMA s.823). A cooperative's objects must be the promotion of the socio-economic interests of its **members** on cooperative principles (Co-operative Societies Act s.2).

**3. There is no conversion route.** CAMA has no procedure for turning incorporated trustees into a cooperative, and the Co-operative Societies Act registers societies on application by their members to the Director of Co-operatives, not by transfer from CAC. If incorporated trustees are dissolved, leftover property must go to another body with **similar objects** or to charity, never to members (CAMA s.850), so the assets cannot simply move into a members' cooperative either.

**4. The name is protected.** Only a registered cooperative may trade under a name containing "Co-operative" without the Director's permission (Co-operative Societies Act s.54).

## Three lawful ways to do both

| Option | How it works | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A separate cooperative alongside the association** | Members form and register their own cooperative with the state cooperative department. It has its own name ("… Co-operative … Society Limited"), officers, bye-laws, books and bank account. The association's money stays with the association. | Churches, mosques, alumni and staff groups whose members want thrift and loans |
| **Welfare within the association, without returns** | The association runs welfare support from its own funds for its objects (for example, helping members in hardship) as set out in its constitution, with no interest, dividends or shares of surplus to members. | Social and religious groups with a welfare arm |
| **A company for a trading arm** | Commercial activity that should make and share profit goes into a limited company. | Associations that want a business (a school, a shop, a guest house) to earn and distribute profit |

::: callout
Keep the money apart. A cooperative set up by members of a church or association must not use the association's bank account, receive its tithes, dues or donations, or be guaranteed by its property unless properly approved and documented. Mixing the two is where most disputes and regulatory trouble start.
:::

## What if we are already doing it?

Many associations already run informal thrift or loans. To put it right:

1. **Stop paying "interest", "dividends" or "returns" to members out of the association's funds.**
2. **Hold a members' meeting** and agree to form a separate cooperative for those who want to save and borrow.
3. **Register the cooperative** with your state cooperative department. See [how to register a cooperative society](/blog/how-to-register-cooperative-society-nigeria/).
4. **Move the savings records and member balances** to the cooperative, with each member's statement agreed and signed.
5. **Update the association's constitution and minutes** to record the change (constitution changes need CAC approval; see [changing trustees, name or constitution](/blog/change-trustees-constitution-incorporated-trustees-nigeria/)).

See also [signs your association should really be a cooperative](/blog/signs-association-should-be-cooperative-nigeria/).

## The short version

Incorporated trustees cannot become a cooperative or share money with members (CAMA s.838), and there is no conversion route. If members want savings, loans or shared surplus, they form a separate cooperative under the state cooperative law, with its own books and accounts, and the association keeps its funds for its cause.

Sources: CAMA 2020 Part F, sections 823, 838 and 850; [Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig120579.pdf), sections 2, 34 and 54.
