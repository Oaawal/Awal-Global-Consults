A **tax clearance certificate (TCC)** confirms that a taxpayer has filed its returns and paid the tax due for the period it covers. In Nigeria it is routinely requested for government contracts and tenders, some licences and permits, bank facilities, and other official processes.

The certificate itself is quick to obtain. What takes time is getting your tax records into the condition that allows it to be issued.

## Who issues it

| Taxpayer | Issued by |
|---|---|
| **Companies** | The **Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)** |
| **Individuals**, including **business name owners**, who are taxed personally | The **State Internal Revenue Service** of the state where they are resident |

::: callout
A business name isn't taxed as a separate entity: its owner pays personal income tax. That's why a business name owner applies to the State Internal Revenue Service, not the NRS.
:::

## What has to be in place first

- **A tax identification number (TIN).** See [how to get your TIN](/blog/tin-retrieval-nigeria-2026/).
- **Returns filed** for the period the certificate covers: typically the previous three years, or the years since you started if the business is newer.
- **Tax paid**: no outstanding liabilities on your record.
- For companies, **financial statements** supporting the returns, and **VAT returns** where the company is registered for VAT.
- Your details must be **consistent** across CAC, the tax authority and your bank.

Our guide to the [Nigeria Tax Act 2025](/blog/nigeria-tax-act-2025-compliance-guide/) explains the filing obligations behind these requirements.

## How to apply

- **Companies** apply to the NRS online. The NRS moved its taxpayer services from TaxPro Max to its new **Rev360** platform from 30 April 2026, so older instructions that mention TaxPro Max are out of date.
- **Individuals and business name owners** apply to their State Internal Revenue Service, through its online portal or office, depending on the state.

When your filings and payments are complete and there are no open issues on your record, the certificate is often issued quickly.

## Why applications get held up

- **Unfiled returns for one or more years**: the most common reason.
- **Outstanding tax**, interest or penalties.
- **An open tax audit or query** on the account.
- **Mismatched details**: a different name or TIN across records.
- **Applying to the wrong authority**: for example, a business name owner applying to the NRS.

::: quote
A TCC isn't something you apply for in a hurry the week a tender closes. Keep filings current all year, and the certificate becomes a formality.
:::

<div class="blog-faq">
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">Can a new business get a TCC?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Yes. A new business can obtain a certificate covering the period since it started, provided its filings for that period are in order.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">I didn't make a profit. Do I still need to file?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">Yes. Filing is required even when no tax is payable, and unfiled years will block a TCC.</p>
</div>
<div class="blog-faq-item">
<p class="blog-faq-q">How long is a TCC valid?</p>
<p class="blog-faq-a">A TCC relates to specific tax years and is generally renewed annually. Check the date it covers against what the requesting organisation needs.</p>
</div>
</div>

## The short version

Companies get their TCC from the NRS (now on Rev360); individuals and business name owners from their State Internal Revenue Service. File every year, pay what's due, keep your details consistent, and the certificate follows quickly.
