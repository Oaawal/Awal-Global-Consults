Getting an expatriate's first CERPAC approved feels like the finish line, and for a lot of employers, it's treated that way. It isn't. A CERPAC is explicitly a **two-year, renewable** permit, which means the process that felt like a one-time achievement is actually the first cycle of a recurring compliance obligation.

Here's what changes, and what stays the same, when renewal time comes around.

## The renewal cycle, and why it's easy to lose track of

::: stat
2 years
The standard validity period of a CERPAC before renewal is required, tied to the underlying Expatriate Quota approval
:::

Because the initial CERPAC process (covered in our [guide to Expatriate Quota and CERPAC](/blog/cerpac-expatriate-quota-nigeria/)) is involved enough that employers focus entirely on getting it approved, the renewal date two years later often arrives with less attention than the original application received. Unlike CAC's annual returns, which recur every single year and stay top of mind, a two-year cycle is long enough that it's genuinely easy to lose track of internally, especially if HR responsibility changes hands in the meantime.

## What the renewal application actually requires

The process has moved fully digital: as of August 2025, CERPAC applications, including renewals, are submitted entirely through the Nigeria Immigration Service's online portal rather than through in-person paper filing.

A renewal application generally needs:

- **An application letter from the employer**, requesting the renewal or replacement of the residence card and formally accepting immigration responsibility for the expatriate, the same undertaking the employer made at initial approval.
- **The expatriate's valid passport**, along with photocopies of the relevant pages, including the existing CERPAC and visa endorsements.
- **Proof of continued employment or business registration**, confirming the expatriate is still filling the role the original quota position covered.
- Other supporting documents as required by the current portal process, which has continued to evolve since the digital transition.

::: callout
Renewal isn't a formality that assumes approval. The employer is re-affirming, formally, that the conditions justifying the original approval still hold. If the expatriate's role, the underlying quota position, or the business's own standing has changed materially, that needs addressing as part of the renewal, not glossed over.
:::

## The part employers actually get wrong: assuming the conditions don't need re-checking

::: quote
A CERPAC renewal isn't just "the same paperwork again." It's confirmation that the qualifying conditions from two years ago, the business's registration, the quota position, the employment relationship, are still genuinely true today.
:::

The conditions that justified the original approval, an active, qualifying business, a genuinely open quota position, an employment relationship matching what was approved, need to be maintained throughout the CERPAC's validity, not just satisfied once at the start. Common issues at renewal time:

- **The expatriate has moved into a different role** than the one the original quota position covered, without a corresponding update to the quota approval.
- **The sponsoring business's own CAC standing has lapsed**, for instance through a missed annual return (see our [guide to CAC annual returns](/blog/cac-annual-returns-deadline-penalty/)), which undermines the "active business" condition the whole arrangement depends on.
- **The quota position itself needs its own renewal or review**, separate from the individual CERPAC, if the underlying approval window has also come up.

## Processing time, and why applying early matters

Once submitted with everything in order, CERPAC processing (renewal or new) typically takes around **14 working days**. That's a manageable window, but only if the renewal application goes in with enough runway before the existing CERPAC expires. An expatriate whose CERPAC lapses while a renewal is still processing is, for that gap, in an irregular immigration status, a real risk for both the individual and the sponsoring employer, entirely avoidable by starting the renewal well ahead of the expiry date rather than at it.

## Treating renewal as a recurring compliance item, not a future problem

The employers who handle this well build CERPAC renewal into the same kind of calendar discipline they'd apply to CAC annual returns or tax filing deadlines, tracked, owned by someone specific, and started early, rather than discovered when an expiry date is suddenly close.
