Nigeria's anti-money-laundering law — the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 — doesn't only apply to banks. It also covers **designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs)**: businesses where large sums can change hands and criminals might try to hide money.

These businesses must register with the **Special Control Unit against Money Laundering (SCUML)** and follow AML rules. See [SCUML registration](/blog/scuml-registration-nigeria/) for the registration process itself.

## Who's covered

Designated businesses include, among others:

- **Real estate agents and dealers**, including property developers.
- **Car dealers** and dealers in automobiles.
- **Dealers in precious metals and stones, and jewellers.**
- Hotels, casinos and similar businesses.
- Accountants, lawyers and other professionals for certain transactions.
- NGOs and non-profits.

If you're not sure whether your business is covered, check SCUML's current list before you assume it isn't.

## What changes day to day

### 1. Know your customer

Before a significant transaction, identify your customer and verify their ID. For a company, identify who really owns and controls it. See the [KYC documentation checklist](/blog/kyc-documentation-checklist-nigeria/).

### 2. Report large cash transactions

::: stat
₦5m / ₦10m
Cash transactions above ₦5 million for an individual, or ₦10 million for a company, must be reported through the channels SCUML prescribes.
:::

**Splitting** a payment into smaller amounts to stay under the threshold is itself an offence — by the customer, and by a business that helps.

### 3. Report suspicious activity

If something about a transaction looks wrong — the customer won't explain where the money came from, pays in unusual ways, or the deal makes no sense — you must file a **suspicious transaction report**. Don't tell the customer you've reported them.

### 4. Keep records

Keep customer identification and transaction records for the period the law requires — generally at least **five years** after the transaction or relationship ends.

::: callout
Banks increasingly ask DNFBPs for their **SCUML certificate** before opening or maintaining accounts. Without it, your banking can stall.
:::

## Practical examples

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| A buyer wants to pay ₦30m cash for a property | Verify ID, record the source of funds, and report the cash transaction |
| A car buyer pays in several cash deposits just under the threshold | Treat it as possible splitting and consider a suspicious transaction report |
| A company buys jewellery through a representative | Identify the company and its owners, and check the representative's authority |
| A customer refuses to give ID | Don't proceed with the transaction |

## The short version

Real estate agents, car dealers and jewellers are designated businesses under Nigeria's AML law. Register with SCUML, verify your customers, report cash transactions above ₦5m (individuals) or ₦10m (companies), report anything suspicious, and keep records.
