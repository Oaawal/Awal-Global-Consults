Do I Need a Lawyer to Register a Company in Nigeria?

Since CAMA 2020, you can register a company yourself on the CAC portal — no lawyer required by law. Here's what self-registration involves, where it goes wrong, and when professional help is worth paying for.

BD Business desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • What self-registration involves
  • Where DIY registrations usually go wrong
  • When doing it yourself makes sense
  • When professional help is worth it
  • The short version

No, the law doesn't require it. Since the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 (CAMA 2020), the person registering a company can sign the statement of compliance themselves, and the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) lets the public register online through its portal. Before 2020, that declaration had to be made by a legal practitioner.

So the real question isn't whether you're allowed to do it yourself. It's whether doing it yourself is the best use of your time — and whether you'll get the structure right.

What self-registration involves

  1. Create an account on CAC's online portal.
  2. Search and reserve a name — see why CAC names get rejected.
  3. Complete the incorporation forms — directors, shareholders, share capital, registered office, business objects.
  4. Prepare or adopt the memorandum and articles of association.
  5. Upload IDs, signatures and photographs for every director and shareholder.
  6. Sign the statement of compliance and pay the fees and stamp duty.
  7. Respond to any queries until approval.

Where DIY registrations usually go wrong

MistakeWhat it costs you later
Wrong or unclear business objectsQueries now; licence or bank problems later
Share capital chosen without thoughtToo little for banks or contracts, or fees and stamp duty paid on capital you didn't need — see how much share capital to register
Shareholding that doesn't reflect the real deal between partnersDisputes, and expensive transfers to fix it
Generic articles that don't cover exits or deadlocksNo agreed process when a partner wants out
Details that don't match IDQueries and delays
Foreign shareholder added without understanding the rulesA company that should have ₦100m share capital — see can a foreigner own a Nigerian company?

A company's structure — who owns what, who decides, and what happens if someone leaves — is set at registration. It's much cheaper to get it right on day one than to change it later.

When doing it yourself makes sense

  • A single-owner company with a simple purpose.
  • You're comfortable with online forms and can respond to queries quickly.
  • You don't need help with structure, shares or partner arrangements.

When professional help is worth it

  • Two or more owners, especially if contributions or roles differ.
  • A foreign shareholder or a diaspora founder.
  • A regulated business that will need licences.
  • You want it done quickly and without queries while you focus on the business.
  • You need post-registration support — bank account, tax registration, annual returns.

Is a company registered by myself any less valid?

No. A company registered directly through CAC's portal is exactly as valid as one registered through an agent.

Do I need a lawyer for a business name?

No. Business names are simpler still — see how to register a business name step by step.

Will I need a lawyer later?

Often, yes — for contracts, shareholder agreements, disputes or property. Registration is just the first legal step.

The short version

You can legally register a company yourself since CAMA 2020. Do it yourself if the company is simple and you have the time; get help when there are partners, foreign shareholders, licences or tight deadlines — because structure mistakes cost far more to fix than to avoid.
