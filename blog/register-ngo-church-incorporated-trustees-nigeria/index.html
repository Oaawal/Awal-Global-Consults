NGOs and trustees

How to Register an NGO, Church or Association in Nigeria (Incorporated Trustees)

How NGOs, churches, mosques, foundations, clubs and associations register as incorporated trustees with CAC: trustees, constitution, the 28-day public notice, the four stages and realistic timelines, and what comes after.

BD Business desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • Who should register this way
  • What you need to prepare
  • The four stages and how long each takes
  • After registration
  • The short version

Churches, mosques, NGOs, foundations, alumni and community associations, clubs and professional bodies are usually registered in Nigeria as incorporated trustees with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). Once registered, the trustees become a body corporate: the organisation can own property, open bank accounts, sign agreements and continue regardless of changes in its membership.

Who should register this way

Incorporated trustees suit organisations set up for religious, educational, literary, scientific, social, development, cultural, sporting or charitable purposes, where the aim is the cause, not profit for members.

If you want to make and share profits, a company is the right structure instead. If you want a non-profit company with members rather than trustees, a company limited by guarantee is the alternative: we can explain the difference for your situation.

What you need to prepare

  • A name for the organisation. Registered names take the form "The Incorporated Trustees of …".
  • At least two trustees. Each trustee provides ID, a passport photograph, contact details, an address and a signature.
  • A constitution setting out the organisation's aims and objects, how trustees are appointed and removed, meetings, finances and how the constitution can be changed.
  • The chairman and secretary of the organisation, and the resolution or minutes appointing the trustees.
  • Trustee declarations, sworn before a Commissioner for Oaths or a Notary Public.

Get the constitution right before you file. It governs how the organisation runs for years, and changing it later means another filing.

The four stages and how long each takes

These timelines come from our own experience. They are not official CAC service times: except the 28-day notice, which is required by law.

StageWhat happensTypical time
1. Name consent and reservationCAC approves and reserves the proposed nameAbout 3 business days
2. Newspaper publicationA notice of the application is published, inviting any objectionsA 28-day window, required by law
3. Declaration of trusteesTrustee declarations are prepared and swornCompleted within the 28 days
4. Final submission and approvalThe application is filed and CAC issues the certificateAbout 3 business days after the notice period
5–6 weeksA realistic total from start to certificate, because the 28-day notice period can't be shortened.

After registration

  • Annual returns. Incorporated trustees file annual returns with CAC, like companies and business names. See CAC annual returns.
  • A bank account in the organisation's registered name.
  • Tax. Registration doesn't make an organisation tax-free automatically. Exemptions depend on the organisation's purpose and activities, and income from trading activities may be taxable.
  • SCUML registration. Non-profit organisations are required to register with SCUML for anti-money-laundering purposes. See SCUML registration in Nigeria.
  • Keeping records: trustee changes, constitution amendments and accounts, filed as required.

Can trustees live outside Nigeria?

Yes. Trustees abroad can provide their details and signatures remotely. Declarations sworn abroad may need to be notarised and authenticated. See signing documents from abroad.

What happens if someone objects during the 28 days?

CAC considers the objection before approving the application. Most objections relate to the name or to disputes within the organisation, which is another reason to settle leadership questions before you apply.

Our church already operates. Is it too late to register?

No. Many organisations register after years of operating. Registering protects property and bank accounts by putting them in the organisation's name rather than an individual's.

The short version

Agree your name, trustees and constitution, then expect about five to six weeks: name consent, a 28-day public notice, trustee declarations and final approval. Then keep up with annual returns, SCUML and your tax position.
