Business Name vs Trademark: Is CAC Registration Enough to Protect My Brand?

CAC registration lets you trade under a name; a trademark gives you exclusive rights to the brand. Here's the difference, why many Nigerian businesses need both, and what happens when they clash.

BD Brand desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • What each one actually gives you
  • Where the gap bites
  • Do you need both?
  • How to protect both
  • The short version

"I've registered my business name with CAC, so my brand is protected." It's one of the most expensive assumptions a Nigerian business can make.

CAC registration and trademark registration do different jobs. One lets you legally trade under a name. The other gives you the exclusive right to use a brand for particular goods or services. Many businesses need both.

What each one actually gives you

CAC registrationTrademark registration
Registered withCorporate Affairs CommissionTrademarks Registry (Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment)
What it doesLets you carry on business under that nameGives you exclusive rights to the mark for the goods or services in its class
What it stopsThe same name being registered again as a business or company nameOthers using the same or a confusingly similar mark as a brand in that class
Covers logos and slogansNo — names onlyYes — names, logos, slogans and more
Lets you take actionLimitedYes — you can act against infringers
DurationOngoing, with annual returnsSeven years, renewable

A CAC certificate proves your business exists. It doesn't make the brand yours to the exclusion of everyone else.

Where the gap bites

  • Someone uses your name on their products. Your CAC registration gives you little to act on, even though you registered first. A registered trademark does.
  • Someone registers your brand as a trademark first. They may then challenge your use of it — even though you registered the business name earlier.
  • Your name clashes with an existing trademark. CAC approval doesn't override trademark rights, and CAC has in the past directed companies to change names that conflict with registered trademarks.
  • You want to expand online. Marketplaces and social platforms generally respond to complaints backed by registered trademarks.

Do you need both?

Your situationWhat we usually suggest
Small local trading under your own name, no plans to build a brandCAC registration is usually enough for now
You sell products or services under a distinctive brand name or logoRegister the business and trademark the brand
You plan to expand, franchise, attract investors or sell onlineTrademark early — before you spend heavily on the brand
Your company name and product brand are differentRegister the company with CAC and trademark the product brand
Trademark the brand before you print the packaging, not after. Rebranding is always more expensive than registering.

How to protect both

  1. Search first. Check the trademark register as well as CAC before you settle on a name — see how to do a trademark search.
  2. Register the business with CAC so you can trade under the name.
  3. File the trademark in the class that covers what you actually sell — see trademark classes explained.
  4. Use the brand consistently, and keep records of when and where you use it.

I registered my business name years ago. Do I have any rights without a trademark?

Possibly. A business that has genuinely traded under a name can build up goodwill, and the law of passing off can protect it. But proving goodwill is slower and harder than relying on a registered trademark.

Does a trademark replace CAC registration?

No. You still need CAC registration to carry on business under the name. The two work together.

Can I trademark my business name if it's a common word?

Very descriptive or generic words are harder to register. Adding a distinctive element — or registering a logo — often helps. A search will show what's realistic.

The short version

CAC lets you trade under a name; a trademark lets you own the brand. If your name is something customers recognise you by, protect it with both — and search before you invest in either.
