Data protection (NDPA)

Does My Small Business Need a Privacy Policy in Nigeria?

If your business collects names, phone numbers, emails or any other personal data, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 expects you to tell people how you use it. Here's what a privacy policy must cover, where to put it, and why a copied template can hurt you.

CD Compliance desk · Awal Global Consults 31 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

On this page

  • What your privacy policy must cover
  • Where it should appear
  • Why a copied template can hurt you
  • A quick self-check
  • The short version

Almost certainly, yes. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) applies to any business that collects or uses personal data — information about an identifiable person. That includes customer names, phone numbers, emails, delivery addresses, staff records, CCTV footage and WhatsApp contacts.

The NDPA requires you to tell people, clearly and before or when you collect their data, what you collect, why, and what you do with it. A privacy policy (or privacy notice) is how you do that.

Size doesn't exempt you. The duty to be transparent applies whether you have 20 customers or 20,000. Registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission is a separate, additional step for businesses that process data on a larger scale — see NDPA compliance.

What your privacy policy must cover

SectionWhat to say
Who you areBusiness name, address and how to contact you about data
What you collectThe actual categories — names, phone numbers, IDs, payment details, CCTV
WhyThe purposes — delivering orders, invoicing, marketing, security
Lawful basisConsent, contract, legal obligation, or legitimate interests
Who you share it withDelivery partners, payment providers, cloud services, regulators
Transfers abroadIf data is stored or processed outside Nigeria — for example on foreign cloud servers
How long you keep itReal retention periods, not "as long as necessary" with nothing else
People's rightsAccess, correction, deletion, objection, withdrawing consent, complaining to the NDPC
Cookies and trackingIf your website uses analytics or advertising cookies

Where it should appear

  • On your website, linked from every page footer and from every form.
  • At the point of collection — a short notice on forms, sign-up pages and WhatsApp catalogues, linking to the full policy.
  • For staff, in employment documents.
  • In-store, if you use CCTV — a visible sign.

Why a copied template can hurt you

A template from another business — or another country — usually describes things you don't do and misses things you do. If something goes wrong, a policy that doesn't match your real practices shows the regulator exactly where the gap is. See what to do after a data breach.

A quick self-check

  1. List every place you collect personal data — website, forms, WhatsApp, phone, in person.
  2. Write down what you collect in each and why.
  3. List every tool or company that sees it — payment providers, couriers, email services, cloud storage.
  4. Decide how long you really need each type of data.
  5. Make sure your policy says all of that, in plain language.

I only sell on WhatsApp and Instagram. Do I need one?

You still collect personal data — names, numbers, addresses. A short privacy notice, linked from your profile or catalogue, is the practical way to meet the duty.

Do I need a cookie banner?

If your website uses non-essential cookies, such as analytics or advertising, you should get consent before setting them and explain them in your policy.

Is a privacy policy all I need?

No — it's the visible part. You also need to keep data secure, honour people's requests, and register with the NDPC if your processing reaches the registration thresholds.

The short version

If you collect personal data, the NDPA expects you to explain clearly what you collect, why, who sees it, how long you keep it and what rights people have. Write a policy that matches what you really do, and put it where people give you their data.
