How Much Share Capital Should a New Nigerian Company Register?

The legal minimum is ₦100,000 for a private company (₦100m with foreign shareholders), but the right figure depends on fees, stamp duty, banks, contracts and licences. Here's how to choose.

BD Business desk · Awal Global Consults Updated 31 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
Reviewed by A.O. Abdullateef, Legal Professional, Founder of Awal Global Consults

Every company registers a share capital: the total value of the shares it issues to its shareholders. It's one of the first decisions in registration, and one of the most misunderstood.

The legal minimum

₦100,000The minimum share capital for a private company under CAMA 2020. Public companies need at least ₦2 million, and any company with a foreign shareholder needs at least ₦100 million.

Why not always use the minimum?

Because share capital affects more than the registration form:

  • Registration cost. CAC fees and stamp duty rise with share capital. Registering more than you need means paying more upfront.
  • Banks and lenders look at share capital as one sign of how serious and substantial a company is.
  • Contracts and tenders sometimes set minimum capital for bidders.
  • Licences. Some regulated sectors require a minimum share capital before they'll license you.
  • Foreign shareholders. The ₦100 million minimum applies the moment any shareholder is foreign. See can a foreigner own a Nigerian company?

Share capital isn't a fee that disappears: it represents what shareholders contribute to the company. But the government fees and stamp duty you pay to register it are a real cost, so choose deliberately.

A practical way to choose

Your situationA sensible approach
Small service or trading business, no special licencesStart modestly: you can increase later
You'll bid for contracts or tendersCheck the capital they expect
Regulated sector (finance, insurance, some logistics and others)Find the regulator's minimum first
Foreign shareholder involvedAt least ₦100 million
Several shareholdersChoose a figure that divides cleanly into the agreed percentages

You can increase it later

Share capital can be increased by resolution and a filing with CAC, paying stamp duty on the increase. Starting modestly and increasing when the business needs it is often more sensible than paying upfront for capital you won't use. See adding a director or shareholder.

How it affects your registration price

Our company registration packages start from ₦75,000, government fees included, for a standard all-Nigerian company. Because government fees and stamp duty rise with share capital (₦10,000 registration plus ₦7,500 stamp duty for every ₦1 million) a higher share capital means a higher quote, always itemised before you pay. See CAC fees 2026.

Do shareholders have to pay the full amount immediately?

Shares can be fully or partly paid. How and when shareholders pay for their shares should be agreed and recorded.

Does high share capital mean I'm personally liable for more?

Shareholders' liability is limited to the amount unpaid on their shares, so it's worth registering a figure that matches what will really be contributed.

Can I reduce share capital later?

Reductions are possible but involve more formal steps than increases, so it's better not to start too high.

The short version

The minimum is ₦100,000 (₦100 million with foreign shareholders). Pick a figure that fits your banks, contracts and licences without paying for capital you don't need, and increase it later as you grow.
