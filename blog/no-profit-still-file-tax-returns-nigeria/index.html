My Business Made No Profit. Do I Still Need to File Tax Returns?

Yes — in Nigeria, companies must file returns every year even with no profit, no sales or a zero tax rate. Here's why, what a nil return means, the small-company rules, and the cost of not filing.

Yes. In Nigeria, the obligation to file a tax return is separate from the obligation to pay tax. A company that made a loss, had no sales, or qualifies for a 0% tax rate must still file its return every year.

Not filing because "there was nothing to pay" is one of the most common — and most expensive — mistakes small businesses make. Under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025, failing to file a return — including a nil return — attracts a penalty of ₦100,000 for the first month and ₦50,000 for each month after, even when the tax due is zero.

Why you still have to file

  • The law requires it. Companies registered in Nigeria must file self-assessment returns with the Nigeria Revenue Service (formerly FIRS) annually, whatever the result.
  • A return is how you show you owe nothing. Without it, the tax authority has no record of your position.
  • Losses can be carried forward. Filing records a loss you may be able to set against future profits.
  • You'll need a tax clearance certificate for contracts, tenders, loans and some licences — and that requires your filings to be up to date. See tax clearance certificates.

What a "nil return" means

A nil return is a return showing no income, no profit or no tax due for the period — and filing it is required by law. It's still a full return: the company's details, the period, and figures — even if they're zeros — usually supported by accounts.

Small companies and the 0% rate

Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, a small company pays 0% company income tax. A company is small if both of these are true:

₦100mAnnual turnover of ₦100 million or less, and total fixed assets of less than ₦250 million.

Small companies are also exempt from capital gains tax and the 4% development levy. But:

  • They still have to file their returns every year — a 0% rate is not an exemption from filing.
  • Professional services businesses — such as consultancies, law, accounting and similar firms — are excluded from the small-company definition whatever their size, so they may pay tax even with a small turnover.
  • If you cross either threshold, the company pays tax at the standard rate. See the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 compliance guide.

Other filings that don't depend on profit

FilingWhy it still applies
CAC annual returnsRequired every year, profit or not — see CAC annual returns
PAYE for staffOwed on salaries you pay, whatever the company's profit
VAT returns (if registered)Monthly filing obligations apply, including nil returns
Withholding taxApplies to qualifying payments you make
Owner's personal income taxYou still pay tax on salary or income you draw

Business names

A business name isn't taxed separately — the owner pays personal income tax on its profits to the state tax authority where they live. The owner's personal return should still be filed every year, profit or not.

Behind on filing?

Don't wait for a demand notice. Get your accounts prepared, file the outstanding returns, and ask about penalties — it's almost always cheaper to regularise early than to deal with assessments and penalties later.

What if the company hasn't started trading yet?

The filing obligation generally starts once the company is registered, so a dormant company should still file. Ask a tax adviser what the first return should cover.

Do I need audited accounts for a nil return?

Requirements depend on the company's size and the tax authority's rules. Many small companies need at least proper financial statements to support the return.

Can I file online?

Yes. Company returns are filed through the tax authority's online platform using the company's TIN.

The short version

No profit doesn't mean no filing. A nil return is required by law, and missing one costs ₦100,000 for the first month and ₦50,000 for each month after. Companies must file every year, small companies at 0% included, and CAC annual returns, PAYE and VAT obligations continue regardless. Filing on time keeps you eligible for tax clearance and avoids penalties.
