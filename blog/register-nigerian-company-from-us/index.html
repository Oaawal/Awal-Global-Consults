Registering a Nigerian Company from the US: The Remote Process

How Nigerians in the United States register a business name or company in Nigeria without travelling — documents, the Nigerian address, signatures, US passports and the ₦100m rule, and US tax points to check.

DD Diaspora desk · Awal Global Consults 30 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

You can register a Nigerian business name or limited company from anywhere in the United States, without flying home. Registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) is done online, and every document it needs can be sent from the US.

The process is almost identical to registering from the UK — see our guide to registering from the UK for the full checklist. This guide covers what's different for Nigerians in the US.

The process, step by step

  1. Talk to us on WhatsApp or by email. Time zones aren't a problem — messages are answered during Lagos business hours, which overlap with US mornings.
  2. Get an itemised quote. Packages start from ₦40,000 for a business name and ₦75,000 for a limited company, government fees included. Add-ons are quoted separately.
  3. Send your details and documents through our secure online application at awalglobal.com.ng/kyc, by email or by WhatsApp.
  4. We reserve the name and file with CAC, and answer any queries.
  5. Receive your certificate by email — typically within 24 hours for a business name and 48–72 hours for a company if there are no queries. Track progress any time at awalglobal.com.ng/track.

What you'll need

  • Valid ID. An international passport works. If you're a Nigerian citizen, use your Nigerian passport or NIN. A US driver's license or state ID may not be accepted.
  • Your US home address — fine as your personal address.
  • A business address in Nigeria — this one must be Nigerian. See do I need a Nigerian address?
  • A clear signature image and a passport photo.
  • Two or three name options and a clear description of what the business will do.

If you hold only a US passport

Many Nigerian-Americans are dual citizens. If you register a company as a Nigerian citizen, the standard process applies. If you — or a co-owner — register a company using only a US passport, it has foreign participation:

A company with any foreign shareholder needs a minimum share capital of ₦100 million. Government fees rise with share capital, so these registrations are quoted individually rather than at our standard company price.

Companies with foreign participation may also need to register with the Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission (NIPC) and obtain other permits, depending on the business. See can a foreigner own 100% of a Nigerian company?

Signing and documents from the US

For CAC registration, a scanned specimen signature is normally all that's needed. If a bank or other institution asks for notarised documents, they're usually notarised by a US notary public and then authenticated by the Nigerian Embassy or a Nigerian consulate in the US. Check what the receiving institution needs before you book anything. More in our guide to signing CAC documents from abroad.

US tax: check before you start

US citizens and US tax residents are generally taxed on worldwide income, and owning or controlling a foreign company can bring IRS information-reporting obligations as well as tax. These rules are detailed and the penalties for missing a form can be significant, so speak to a US tax adviser about your own situation before you register.

Do I need a Nigerian bank account?

Not to register. To trade, a company will want a corporate account in Nigeria. Banks differ on whether directors abroad can complete onboarding remotely, so ask early.

Can my relative in Nigeria run the business for me?

Yes. Appointing a trusted person in Nigeria as a director or manager is common. For specific tasks only, a limited power of attorney may be enough.

How do I pay from the US?

You'll receive an itemised quote first, then payment details once you're happy with it.

The short version

From the US, the registration itself is fully remote and usually takes one to three days. The two things to get right are which passport you register with — because of the ₦100m foreign-participation rule — and your US tax reporting.
